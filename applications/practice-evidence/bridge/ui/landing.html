The problem

AI role-play is easy to buy. Its scores are hard to trust.

Practice works. People who rehearse a difficult conversation handle the real one better. AI makes that rehearsal available on demand, at any hour.

The feedback is where it breaks down. An AI score usually arrives with no way to check it. Did the learner actually say what the AI claims? Who decided what "good" means? Would a person agree? When a learner asks "why?", the honest answer is often "the model said so."

That isn't good enough for anything that goes into a development plan, a coaching conversation or a readiness decision.

What you get

Practice people use. Feedback you can defend.

  • Practice that feels like the real conversation

    Each scenario describes the other person: who they are, what they want, what they know and how they react. Learners talk it through as many times as they need.

  • Feedback grounded in their own words

    Every level the AI proposes has to quote what the learner actually said, and we check each quote word for word. Learners also see exactly what the next level asks for.

  • A person signs off, every time

    Nothing counts as evidence until a qualified reviewer confirms or sets every level. The reviewer can't be the learner or anyone who assessed the session, and the rubric must be approved by a reviewer who didn't write it.

  • Records people can keep and check

    Every result is a digitally signed record. Learners can download theirs and verify it on their own, ask for another review, or delete the conversation text.

How it works

From first sign-in to a result you can stand behind

  1. Set up your team

    Your admin signs in with Interego and adds people as authors, reviewers and learners.

  2. Write a scenario

    An author describes the situation, the person to practice with, and a rubric of criteria with clear levels. They also decide whether it's feedback only or scored with a pass mark. AI can write a first draft, but your author makes the decisions.

  3. Approve the rubric

    A reviewer who didn't write the scenario approves it. Until that happens, results on it can't leave as evidence.

  4. Practice and get feedback

    The learner says whether anyone helped them prepare, then holds the conversation and ends it when ready. An AI judge proposes a level for each criterion, quoting the learner. The platform checks every quote and works out the result.

  5. Review and keep

    A reviewer confirms or adjusts each level and gives reasons for any change. The learner sees the final result and can ask for a second review. They can also download their signed record or export it in the xAPI format used by learning record stores.

Who it's for

Built for everyone in the loop

  • L&D and enablement leaders

    Practice your people actually use, with results you can defend to a manager, a learner or an auditor.

  • Authors

    Write a scenario in plain language, let AI draft it if you like, try it yourself, then publish. Each edit becomes a new version, so earlier results stay tied to the rubric they used.

  • Reviewers

    A prioritized queue that puts first the sessions most likely to need a person: quotes that didn't check out, AI assessments that disagree, results close to the pass mark. Confirm in one step, or set your own levels with a reason.

  • Learners

    A safe place to rehearse. You see the rubric up front, your feedback quotes your own words, and you can ask for another review. Your record stays yours.

Trust

Built for trust, not just for scoring

  • Signed records

    Every step is saved as a new signed version. Nothing is overwritten. Anyone can confirm a record hasn't been changed since it was signed, including from a downloaded copy.

  • People hold their own record

    Learners can download their complete signed record at any time and ask for a different reviewer to look again. They can also delete the conversation text from the workspace. The result's fingerprints stay, so their own copy still checks out.

  • AI declared and checked

    Every AI reply and every AI assessment shows which model produced it and who chose that model. The AI must quote the learner, and a quote that doesn't match, word for word, doesn't count. The AI never sees the weights or the pass mark, and it never calculates the score.

  • Nothing invented

    If no AI is available, we say so; we never fake a reply. If an AI's answer breaks the rules, nothing is saved and nothing is patched up. Where nothing was checked, the result says "nothing checked", never "verified".

  • Your data, in your store

    Records live in a data store (Interego calls it a pod), not inside our app's own database. Your workspace can use the Interego-hosted store or one your organization runs, chosen at setup.

  • Encrypted at rest

    Conversation text, AI assessments and reviewer notes are encrypted before they're stored. No stored record names the learner.

  • Small numbers stay private

    Results pages hide any count from 1 to 4, so nobody can work out how one person did.

  • Rubrics are open

    Learners can read the full rubric before they start.

What this does and doesn't prove
  • Some facts stay visible to anyone who can read the store, even with encryption: that a record exists, when it changed and how many versions it has, and its review status. The IDs of the scenario's authors, the reviewer who approved the rubric and the reviewer of a session are also visible.
  • A signature shows a record wasn't changed. It can't show that someone with write access to the store didn't delete the newest version. If a record matters to you, keep your own download.
  • The service holds the encryption key. If you run Practice Evidence yourself, you hold it.
Works where you already are

Use it from Claude or ChatGPT

Add the Interego connector to Claude or ChatGPT and sign in once with your Interego account. You're the same person in the chat app and in the web app.

  • No AI provider key needed. Your own assistant can draft a scenario or play the other person in a practice session. Practice Evidence gives it the exact instructions. The record notes that your assistant did the work, and reviewers can see that.
  • Ask in plain language. Your assistant can list the scenarios, run a practice session with you, show your feedback and download your signed record. Try the examples under the setup steps.
  • Assessment stays independent. You can't assess your own session. Feedback comes from your workspace's built-in AI if it has one, or from an independent judge your admin appoints. You can also use your own AI provider key for one request, and the record notes that too.
  • Decisions happen in the web app. Approving a rubric, signing off a review, changing someone's role and withdrawing a review all put a person's name behind a result. These are done in the Practice Evidence web app, not from a chat assistant, unless your admin allows otherwise.

Set it up in four steps

  1. In Claude or ChatGPT, open Settings, then Connectors, then Add custom connector.

  2. Paste the Interego connector address: https://relay.interego.xwisee.com/mcp

  3. Sign in with your Interego account when asked.

  4. Give your assistant your workspace's address (copy it from Help → Use from Claude or ChatGPT) and tell it what you want to do.

Honest limits

When Practice Evidence isn't the right fit (yet)

  • You need a certificate, badge or credential.

    Practice Evidence records practice. It never issues a credential, and every record says "practice".

  • You want AI to grade without a person.

    Here a reviewer signs off every result before it counts. That is the point of the product.

  • You want to open practice to the public, customers or anonymous users.

    There is no automatic screening yet of what learners type or of what authors publish. Use it with your own colleagues.

  • Your learners must sign in with company SSO, email or your LMS.

    Today people sign in with an Interego account (passkey) or a crypto wallet.

  • You need automatic sync to your LMS or learning record store.

    We produce a standard xAPI statement for each reviewed result, and you send it to your learning record store yourself. SCORM, LTI grade passback and launching from an LMS aren't supported.

  • You need voice, avatars, or an interface in a language other than English.

    Practice is text-based. Replies appear when complete, not word by word. The interface hasn't had an accessibility review yet.

  • You need transcripts deleted automatically after a set period.

    Learners and admins can delete conversation text, but there are no automatic retention windows yet.

  • You need an overall written summary for each learner.

    Feedback is given per criterion.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Who owns the data?

Records are kept in a data store your organization chooses at setup: the Interego-hosted store or one you run. They aren't locked inside our app. Each learner can download their complete signed record at any time and verify it without us, and they can delete the conversation text from the workspace.

How is AI used?

In three places, each labeled. AI can draft a scenario for an author to edit. AI plays the other person in the conversation. And an AI judge proposes a level for each criterion, quoting the learner. AI never decides how a scenario is used, never sees the weights or the pass mark, never calculates a score and never has the final say.

Which AI, and do we need an API key?

No key is required. Your workspace can use a built-in AI model if one is configured. People can also use their own Claude or ChatGPT through the Interego connector, or their own AI provider key for a single request. That key is used once and never stored. Every reply and assessment shows which model produced it and who chose that model.

What if the AI gets it wrong?

That's what the design is for. A quote that doesn't match the learner's words doesn't count, and that criterion stays at its lowest level until a reviewer sets it. A qualified person reviews every result before it counts. A learner who disagrees can ask for another review, up to twice per session, and it must come from a different reviewer. Both reviews are kept.

Is it secure? Who can read what?

Conversation text, AI assessments and reviewer notes are encrypted before they're stored, and no stored record names the learner. Inside the workspace, a session can be read by the learner, reviewers, admins, and a judge who assessed it. Authors see scenario results only as counts, with small numbers hidden. Every action is signed, so changes can be detected. When you sign in with Interego, the Interego sign-in service sees each action it signs for you, including the text of your lines. The Practice Evidence service holds your sign-in session for up to an hour.

What is an Interego account?

It's your sign-in for Practice Evidence and other Interego apps. You create it once with a passkey (your device's fingerprint, face or PIN) or a crypto wallet. There is no password and no email. The same account works in the web app and inside Claude or ChatGPT, so you're one person everywhere. When you act through it, Interego confirms it's you, and your records note that.

Is a result a certification?

No. Every record and export says "practice". Whether the learner said they had help preparing, and which attempt this was, are both recorded with the result.

Does it work with our LMS or learning record store?

For every reviewed result on an approved rubric, Practice Evidence builds a standard xAPI statement (the format learning record stores use) and gives it to you to send. It also lists what the statement leaves out, such as the transcript and the reviewer's reasons. It doesn't post to your learning record store for you, and it doesn't support SCORM, LTI or launching from an LMS.

Can anyone see how an individual did?

Not from the reports. On results pages, any group with 1 to 4 people shows only its total. AI judge statistics are visible without signing in, but only as totals, with small numbers hidden the same way.

Pricing depends on your team and setup. Contact us and we'll walk you through it.
