What Practice Evidence is

Practice Evidence lets your people rehearse difficult workplace conversations with an AI role-player and get feedback against a rubric your team writes. The AI proposes levels and quotes the learner's own words, the platform checks every quote, and a qualified reviewer signs off, so every result is a signed record you can defend and the learner can keep.

It records practice, not a credential.

Before you start

  • You need a modern browser and an Interego account. Create it during your first sign-in with a passkey (your device's fingerprint, face or PIN) or a crypto wallet. There is no password and no email.
  • Your admin gives you a role. Send them your Member ID (account menu → Your Member ID → Copy).

Other ways to sign in: a crypto wallet, or signing each step manually with your own key (advanced, from the sign-in menu).

For learners

  1. Sign in and open Practice.

  2. Choose a scenario. Read what you're practicing, your part, the situation, and the rubric.

  3. Say whether anyone helped you prepare, then press Start practice.

  4. Type your lines. The other person replies. Finish when you're ready, or when they signal the end and you agree.

  5. Get your assessment. Read each level, your highlighted words, and what the next level asks for.

  6. Wait for a reviewer's sign-off, or request another review if you disagree (up to twice, with a different reviewer).

  7. Download your record. Optionally share it as an xAPI learning record, or delete the conversation text.

  8. Practice again whenever you like: Start again is on the session page.

Tips

Each scenario has a line limit per session. One session can be assessed up to 5 times. If you're an author, your own sessions on your scenario are test runs and never count.

For authors

  1. Open Write a scenario.

  2. Optional: start from an AI draft, with the workspace's AI or your own AI. The AI never chooses how the scenario is used or scored.

  3. Fill in the scenario and the other person: who they are, what they want, what they know, how they react, how they open, and when the conversation ends.

  4. Make the two decisions: feedback only, or scored with a pass mark; and how clear-cut good performance is. A pass mark on situational or unsettled work needs a written reason.

  5. Write the rubric: up to 12 criteria, each with 2 to 6 levels, lowest first.

  6. Say who helped write it.

  7. Save a draft, try it yourself, then publish. Ask a reviewer to approve the rubric.

  8. Editing: every change creates a new version. While a new draft is unpublished, learners can't start that scenario.

  9. Results: counts per scenario, with small groups hidden. Reviewers and admins also see each change a reviewer made and the reason for it.

For reviewers

  1. Approve rubrics you didn't write (Practice → a scenario → Approve this rubric).

  2. Work through To review. It's ordered by what most needs a person, and each item shows why.

  3. Choose a level for every criterion. Give a reason wherever yours differs from the AI's or a quote didn't match.

  4. Blind checks: choose your levels before the AI's are revealed.

  5. Submit with Confirm all levels or Sign off my levels.

  6. Re-reviews requested by learners come first, and you won't get one you reviewed before.

  7. Use Results and AI judge quality to spot patterns.

For admins

  1. Team: add and remove people by Member ID, and choose their roles. Have at least two reviewers, so a rubric can be approved by someone who didn't write it and a re-review can go to a different person.

  2. Appoint a judge if your workspace has no built-in AI.

  3. Withdraw a review for cause (the learner sees your reason), or delete a session's conversation text (a reason is required). Both are done in the web app, from the session page.

  4. Monitor AI judge quality.

For appointed judges

  1. Connect Claude or ChatGPT (see Use from Claude or ChatGPT).

  2. Get the judge hand-off for a session you've been given, assess it, and save your assessment.

  3. You can't assess your own practice. Your assessment is checked quote by quote, and a reviewer has the final say.

Use from Claude or ChatGPT

Add the Interego connector to Claude or ChatGPT and sign in once with your Interego account. You're the same person in the chat app and in the web app.

  1. In Claude or ChatGPT, open Settings, then Connectors, then Add custom connector.

  2. Paste the Interego connector address: https://relay.interego.xwisee.com/mcp

  3. Sign in with your Interego account when asked.

  4. Give your assistant your workspace's address (below) and tell it what you want to do.

Your workspace's address

the address of this site, up to (not including) /ui/help

Things to ask

Learners

  • "List the practice scenarios."
  • "Start a practice session on 'Following up on a missed deadline' and play the other person."
  • "Show me my feedback."
  • "Download my signed record."

Authors

  • "Draft a practice scenario about a project manager telling a client sponsor that two defects block the payment flow."
  • "Show me the results for my scenario."

Reviewers

  • "What's waiting for my review, and why is each one near the top?"
  • "Show me how often the AI judge agrees with reviewers."

Appointed judges

  • "Get the judge hand-off for this session, assess it, and save my assessment." Paste the session link you were sent.

Which steps stay in the web app

Approving a rubric, signing off a review, changing someone's role and withdrawing a review all put a person's name behind a result. These are done in the Practice Evidence web app, not from a chat assistant, unless your admin allows otherwise.

Your data and privacy

What's encrypted and what stays visible

Conversation text, AI assessments and reviewer notes are encrypted before they're stored, and no stored record names the learner. Even so:

  • Some facts stay visible to anyone who can read the store, even with encryption: that a record exists, when it changed and how many versions it has, and its review status. The IDs of the scenario's authors, the reviewer who approved the rubric and the reviewer of a session are also visible.
  • A signature shows a record wasn't changed. It can't show that someone with write access to the store didn't delete the newest version. If a record matters to you, keep your own download.
  • The service holds the encryption key. If you run Practice Evidence yourself, you hold it.

Who can see a session

Inside the workspace, a session can be read by the learner, reviewers, admins, and a judge who assessed it. Authors see scenario results only as counts, with small numbers hidden.

Downloading and checking your record

On a session page, open What you can do next and choose Download my record. The file holds every saved version with its signature, and explains how to check each one on your own.

Deleting conversation text

Delete conversation text removes the words from the workspace. Levels and fingerprints stay, so a copy you downloaded still checks out. It can't be undone, so download your record first.

What the Interego sign-in service sees

When you sign in with Interego, the Interego sign-in service confirms each step for you, and it sees what it signs, including the text of your lines. Practice Evidence never sees your passkey.

How long you stay signed in

Sessions last up to an hour. Signing out clears this browser tab. Your Interego session itself stays valid until it expires, within that hour.

Troubleshooting

Each message in the app says what happened and what to do. The most common ones:

Please sign in to continue
Sign in with Interego (or another way from the sign-in menu), then try the step again.
Your session has ended
Sessions last up to an hour. Sign in again to pick up where you left off.
That took a little too long
Each step has to be confirmed within a few minutes. Try again.
You don't have access to this
Your role doesn't include this step. Copy your Member ID from the account menu and send it to your admin.
Please do this in the web app
Approving rubrics, signing off reviews, changing roles and withdrawing reviews happen in the web app, not from a chat assistant.
Someone else needs to do this
Reviews and approvals must come from someone other than the learner, the author or whoever assessed the session. Ask a colleague who can.
AI isn't available for this step
This workspace has no built-in AI, or its AI is only for people on your team. Use Let my AI play the other person, or ask your admin.
Please wait a moment
You've reached an hourly limit, or many people are signing in at once. Wait for the time shown, then try again.
This page is out of date
Something changed since you opened the page. Press Refresh, check, and try again. Your unsent line is kept.
The AI didn't respond properly
Nothing was saved. Try again in a moment, or ask for a second opinion.
We couldn't reach this workspace's storage
Nothing was saved. Try again; if it keeps happening, tell your admin.
Some records here can't be opened
The records were written with a key this service doesn't hold. Contact your admin.
Finish the conversation first
Some steps follow others: finish the conversation before it's assessed, and have it assessed before it's reviewed.
Something went wrong
Nothing was saved. Try again in a moment.

If something keeps going wrong, open Technical details under the message, copy what it says, and send it to us.

Glossary

Scenario
A practice situation: who you are, who you're talking to, and how it will be assessed.
Rubric
The criteria and levels this practice is assessed against. You can read it before you start.
Criterion
One thing the rubric looks at, such as "Names the constraint".
Level
How far a criterion was met, from lowest to highest. Each level says what it looks like.
Weight and points
Only for scored scenarios: how much each criterion counts, and how many points each level is worth.
Session
One practice conversation and its result. Sessions are counted per person per scenario; starting over with a different sign-in (a new wallet, say) restarts the count.
Test run
Your own sessions on your scenario are never reviewed, counted or shared.
Blind check
A random sample of sessions is reviewed blind, to measure how independent reviews are. The reviewer chooses levels before seeing the AI's.
Provisional
Not yet reviewed by a person.
Final
A qualified reviewer stood behind every level.
Fingerprint
A unique code for the exact text. Any later change would produce a different fingerprint.
Member ID
Your unique ID in Interego. Send it to your admin so they can give you a role.
Storage
Where this workspace keeps its records. Interego calls this a pod: a data store your organization chooses.
AI agent (not a person)
Marks a member as software. Software can never approve a rubric or sign off a review.
AI judge
Proposes a level for each criterion, quoting the learner. Never calculates the score and never has the final say.
AI judge quality
How often each AI judge's levels match what reviewers decided, by criterion. This measures agreement, not accuracy: reviewers usually see the AI's levels first.
Confidence score (Brier)
How well the AI's stated confidence matched outcomes. Lower is better.
Track record
Shown for information. It never lets an AI skip a human review.
Learning record (xAPI)
xAPI is the standard format learning record stores accept. We create it; you send it.
