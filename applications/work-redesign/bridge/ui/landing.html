The problem

AI changes to work are being decided on guesswork.

Most “what can AI do for us” exercises end in a slide: a list of tasks, a savings number and a go-ahead. Nobody can say where the numbers came from. The people who do the work were never asked. The agent was trusted before it had proved anything. And six months later, nobody measures whether it worked.

Work Redesign replaces the slide with a record.

  • {{icon:check}}Every task is described by the people who do it.
  • {{icon:check}}Every number has a source.
  • {{icon:check}}Every approval comes from someone independent.
  • {{icon:check}}Every result is measured by someone who didn’t make the prediction.

How it works

From first sign-in to a measured result

Each step leaves a signed record. Nothing moves forward until the right people have signed it.

Read the full guide {{icon:arrow-right:sm}}

  1. Set up your workspace

    Sign in with your Interego account. Your admin adds your team and their roles, your AI agents (each with an accountable owner) and the types of task your work falls into.

  2. Map how the work is really done

    Describe a process by hand, or have your own AI assistant draft a map from your procedure document. Every quote it cites is checked against the document. Each person named on a task then confirms the description or disputes it with a note. A disputed task is blocked from any change until it has been revised and confirmed.

  3. Sort each task by how predictable it is

    Answer seven plain questions: Do the same steps give the same result? Can the output be checked? How soon do you know it went well? What’s the worst case? A published rule places the task as Routine, Needs expertise, Exploratory or Unstable, and that decides what kind of change is allowed.

    RoutineNeeds expertiseExploratoryUnstable

  4. Propose and price the change

    Choose who does each task: a person; a person with AI assisting; AI drafts and a person decides; AI acts and a person checks; or AI on its own, with earned permission. Enter your ranges, and a simulation shows low, likely and high weekly impact, payback and the inputs that move the result most. Exploratory work gets small, safe-to-fail trials instead of a price.

  5. Approve, then measure

    An approver with no hand in the plan approves or declines it. After the measurement period has ended, someone who neither prepared nor approved it records what happened. Work Redesign shows whether the result landed inside the predicted range, and your track record grows with every decision.

Honest limits

When Work Redesign isn’t the right tool

  • {{icon:warning}}

    Work nobody can measure

    If there’s no signal anyone can track, Work Redesign won’t put a value on it. Run small trials instead.

  • {{icon:warning}}

    One-off decisions

    Track records and AI permissions need repetition. A decision you’ll make once doesn’t benefit.

  • {{icon:warning}}

    Changes you won’t measure

    If nobody will record what happened, the decision will read “Approved, results not yet measured” for good, and Work Redesign will say so.

  • {{icon:warning}}

    When you want the tool to decide for you

    Work Redesign doesn’t generate options or write plans. Your team proposes, and Work Redesign checks, prices and records.

  • {{icon:warning}}

    Teams that must not see each other’s records

    Everyone in a workspace can read all of its records, including dispute notes and valuations. Use separate workspaces for them.

  • {{icon:warning}}

    If you need these today

    Importing from an HR or learning system, reading PDF or Word files directly (use plain text or Markdown for now), structured interviews, or withdrawing a decision once it’s submitted (an approver declines it instead).

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What exactly is Work Redesign?

A workspace for deciding which parts of your team’s work an AI agent should take on, and under how much human supervision. You map the work, sort each task by how predictable it is, propose and price a change, get it approved by someone independent, and measure what happened. Everything is kept as a signed record.

Who owns our data, and where is it stored?

Your records live in the data store your workspace is set up with, as signed files, not in a closed database. Anyone named in a record, or who signed one, can copy every record about their work to their own storage or download it.

Does Work Redesign use AI? Does it send our documents to an AI model?

Work Redesign runs no AI model. It writes no text and invents no numbers. If you want help drafting a process map, it gives you instructions to pass to your own AI assistant, which then works under your own agreement with that provider. Work Redesign checks every quote the draft relies on against your document. Your document’s text is stored with the record only if you choose to store it, or if you record the map from a chat assistant.

What is an Interego account, and do I need one?

Interego is the identity and records layer Work Redesign is built on. Your Interego account is how you sign in: you create it in about two minutes with a passkey (Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello or a security key), with no password and no credit card. The same account connects Claude or ChatGPT. You can sign with a crypto wallet instead if you have one.

How secure is it?

There are no passwords. Every action is signed, and only people your admin has added can read a workspace. {{encryption_short}}, and records changed outside the app are detected and not used.

Two things to know. First, everyone in a workspace can read all of its records. Second, signing in with your Interego account means you trust Interego, and whoever runs your Work Redesign service, with that sign-in for up to a day. Signing out on the page doesn’t cut that short. “Sign out everywhere” on your Interego dashboard ends the day-long part; the hour-long part runs out on its own.

Can an AI agent approve things, or give itself permission?

No. Only people approve, and the approver can’t be anyone who prepared the plan, anyone whose work it changes, or the owner of an agent it puts on the work. An agent gets permission to work on its own only from an approver who isn’t its owner, and only after it has earned it.

The bar is at least 20 pieces of work that people reviewed and accepted at 90% or better, from at least 5 different reviewers, and at least 20 pieces the agent itself called ready, also accepted at 90% or better with no critical failure among them. Critical work needs a much longer clean record. Permissions are for AI agents only. They are never given to people.

What if someone disagrees with how their work is described?

They dispute it, with a note. The task is then blocked from any change until it’s revised and confirmed again, and the revision shows which disputes it answers. When a plan assigns someone new work, they can object; an approver can only approve over an objection by naming it.

How do you calculate the savings, and why should finance trust them?

Every input is a range (low, likely, high) with its source. A simulation of 5,000 draws gives a low, likely and high result for the weekly impact, the net present value, the payback month and the chance the change pays off. The cash figure counts freed hours as savings only where the work is left unfilled; the redeployed figure is shown next to it.

You also see which inputs move the result most, flagged where they’re unmeasured estimates. The same inputs always give the same result.

What happens after a decision is approved?

It reads “Approved, results not yet measured” until the measurement period you declared has ended. Then someone who neither prepared nor approved it records what happened, and whether the change was kept, reversed or stopped early. Work Redesign grades the result against the prediction, adjusted for ramp-up, and adds it to your track record. Before-and-after results are always labelled “no comparison group”.
