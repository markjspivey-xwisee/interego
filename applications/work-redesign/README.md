# Work Redesign — decide which work moves to agents, price it honestly, and keep a record people hold

> **Vertical application of Interego — NOT part of the protocol, NOT a reference implementation of the protocol.** One specific use case among many possible. The Interego protocol does not depend on any of this; multiple alternative verticals could exist over the same substrate. Vocabulary in this document (`wrd:` namespace) is vertical-scoped, non-normative, and lives in [`ontology/wrd.ttl`](ontology/wrd.ttl).
>
> Status: Working Draft. Layer: application-over-L3. No L1/L2/L3 ontologies are extended.

## What it does

An operations lead maps how a team's work is actually done. They can capture it by hand, or paste an SOP and let their **own** AI agent draft a task map, where every claim cites a verbatim span that the platform checks. They review that draft in the same editable table before signing it.

Everyone named on a task ratifies or disputes its description with their own signature, through a link to that task. A disputed description is revised, and the revision carries the dispute it answers. Each task is then classified by how knowable its cause and effect is: Evident, Knowable, Emergent or Turbulent. The classification comes from seven evidence questions, and the people named on the task can ratify or dispute it. The regime decides what may change:

- Only Evident work may move to an agent acting within an earned grant.
- Knowable work is valued only when its classification was answered by an approver or by a person named on the task, or ratified by everyone named on it (expertise itself is not checked).
- Emergent work changes only through safe-to-fail probes, which are never priced.
- Turbulent work is stabilised first.

The operator proposes who does each task: a person, an agent, or both. They value the change with ranges. Every input says where it came from, and a seeded Monte Carlo gives P10, P50 and P90 for two figures:

- a cash figure, in which freed person-hours count only where the work is left unfilled;
- a figure in which freed hours are redeployed at their current cost.

An approver countersigns or declines. From none of their keys may the approver have proposed the plan, requested its valuation or submitted the decision; nor may they be a person whose work the decision changes, or the accountable principal of an agent it puts on the work. Approval re-checks everything submission checked. After the measurement window has actually ended, someone who neither prepared nor approved the decision records what happened. The platform grades that result against the predicted band, scaled for the declared ramp.

Agents earn a revocable grant on one registered kind of task only from runs that people reviewed and accepted. Anyone named in a record, or who signed one, can take a copy of every record about their work.

Every record is a signed Interego context descriptor on the operator's pod. Given a record key, each record graph the bridge writes is sealed on the pod (graphs written there before the key stay plaintext, and the read model counts them), and reads through the bridge need a signature from an admin or a registered key. The platform generates no text and invents no numbers. Every refusal is a typed HTTP refusal.

A person signs either with a key they hold (an EIP-191 wallet), or by signing in with their Interego account, from Claude, ChatGPT or this bridge's web UI: then the Interego relay signs for them, and the bridge takes the request as the user their signed-in agent belongs to. That second way is **relay-attested, not user-held**: whoever holds the relay's delegation key, or controls the identity host, can act here as any person, and every record made that way says `relay-delegated` (see **Signing in with an Interego account**).

## How this differs

- **Classify the kind of work before choosing a method, and let the method change what is allowed.** A published decision table (`wrd:RuleR0` … `wrd:RuleR4`) places each task from signed evidence answers. These include whether a reviewer can check the output and how soon feedback arrives. The rules, the regime each one yields, each regime's method and its permitted levels are read from `wrd.ttl` at runtime. The conditions each rule tests are code (`classifyTask` in `src/work.ts`) that follows the rule's published `wrd:condition` text. The regime then limits the allocation levels. Allocation is refused until every task in the workflow is classified; answers that match no rule are refused (R0) rather than defaulted, and an unknown checkability or feedback speed keeps otherwise-Evident work at Knowable. An agent is put on a task only once everyone named on it has ratified its classification: their silence is not assent.
- **People and agents are one workforce, at levels we define.** The levels are: a person; a person with an agent assisting; an agent drafts and a person decides; an agent acts and a person audits; an agent acts within an earned grant. Every person and registered agent named on a task signs for its description. A person named to decide on or audit an agent's work can acknowledge that role or object to it, and an approver must name the objection to approve over it.
- **Ranges, not points, with the source of every number.** Every input is low / likely / high, with its basis: measured, from a document, reported by the performer, an estimate, or not applicable (zero, for a cost the state does not have). The result gives two figures side by side: cash, and with freed hours redeployed. It also shows freed person-hours with what happens to them, a cost breakdown, a sensitivity ranking that marks unmeasured estimates, and pilot size by the rule of three.
- **Predictions stay Hypothetical until an accountable person countersigns, and stay "approved; prediction unmeasured" until the world answers.** A measured outcome supersedes the countersigned record on the same graph. It is graded against the ramp-adjusted band, and an interval that straddles a band edge is inconclusive. Before-after measurements are labelled "no comparison group" wherever they appear.
- **The people described hold, ratify, dispute and revise their record.** A disputed description blocks changes to that task until it is revised and ratified again. Each person can copy every record about them to a pod they name, or download it. The copies keep the platform's original signature, and a download verifies offline (see **Sign and verify from a terminal**).
- **Grants are earned, scoped, revocable and for agents only, each with an accountable principal.** Earning a grant on one registered kind of task needs all of the following:
  - at least 20 person-reviewed runs on the agent's currently declared model, of which a person accepted at least 90%;
  - at least 5 distinct registered reviewers the admin attests are different people;
  - at least 20 runs the agent itself called ready, of which a person accepted at least 90%, with no critical failure among them.

  How often the agent's own label agreed with its reviewer is reported beside these, never in their place: an agent that truthfully calls most of its work reworked earns nothing. A run under a grant is accepted only on a task that an approved decision put at "agent acts within grant" for that agent, only while the task's current classification still permits it, and only when the agent calls the run ready itself. Revocation is a superseding Retracted record. Revocation and model changes are checked on every use, and work a decision put under a revoked grant is shown with no authorised performer until a new decision changes it. People are never given enforced grants.
- **Capture with cited evidence that the platform checks and never repairs, and with any assistance declared.** A quote that does not appear in the document is flagged `unverified`, never corrected. Capture from a document requires the signer to declare who drafted the map (a person, an agent, or "not declared"); manual capture records no drafting declaration, and neither declaration is verified.

## Built on

- **Cynefin** (Snowden; Cognitive Edge): the four regimes, and safe-to-fail probes with amplify and dampen conditions. The regimes are `skos:closeMatch` to `agp:Evident` … `agp:Turbulent`.
- **The rule of three** (Hanley & Lippman-Hand): pilot sizing. Zero critical failures in ⌈3 / ceiling⌉ runs bounds the rate below the ceiling at 95%, one-sided.
- **xoshiro128\*\*** (Blackman & Vigna): the seeded generator. The seed is `sha256(canonicalJson({planIri, inputs}))`, so the same plan and inputs give the same bands and the same record.
- **Triangular three-point estimates**, and NPV with a linear ramp and a monthly discount rate.
- **Interego's judgment kit** (`applications/_shared/judgment-kit/loop.ts`): calibration, attestation, reputation and the earned-autonomy rule.

## When not to use this

- Work with no signal anyone can measure. The tool refuses to value it; use probes instead.
- One-off decisions that will not recur. Calibration and grants need repetition.
- Changes nobody will measure. The record would stay "approved; prediction unmeasured" forever, and the tool will say so.

## Run it

**The offline demo** walks the whole lifecycle against an in-memory pod. It runs deterministically and needs no network:

```bash
npx tsx applications/work-redesign/demo/run.ts            # prints every step; writes demos/output/wrd-<time>.md (gitignored)
npx tsx applications/work-redesign/demo/run.ts --serve    # then keeps the bridge and web UI up at http://127.0.0.1:6100/ui
npx tsx applications/work-redesign/demo/run.ts --serve --port=6197
```

The demo drives the bridge the way a Path-A agent does. It reads `/affordances?format=jsonld`, calls only the targets it declares, and signs each envelope with a persona key; every read it makes is a signed read by the admin. It seals every record to a record key derived from a published seed, and checks that the pod holds only sealed graphs. Along the way it asserts every refusal, `R-A` to `R-AV`, with its status. Those refusals cover:

- an unsigned read, and a signed read by a key that is neither an admin nor registered;
- allocation before classification, and an agent placed before its task's classification is ratified;
- a fabricated quote;
- a disputed description;
- self-countersigning from either of the submitter's keys, a proposer countersigning from their second key, and an agent's principal countersigning;
- a subject countersigning;
- approving over an assignment nobody acknowledged, without naming it;
- the approver grading the decision they approved;
- a replayed envelope;
- an outcome recorded before its window ends;
- an unearned grant, a grant to a person, and a grant to an agent whose own labels agreed with its reviewers every time but whose work a person accepted once in 20;
- a run under a grant that the agent itself calls reworked;
- every use of a revoked grant;
- an altered run envelope;
- a stale envelope;
- a holder copy to the platform pod;
- a tampered record (its sealed graph changed on the pod).

The demo doubles the network (the pod), time, the people, and what those people report:

- **Time** is a clock that moves only when the walk calls `wrd.demo_advance_clock`, a demo-only affordance, so a nine-week window can pass.
- **The people** are persona keys derived from published seeds and held by the demo process. So is the record key.
- **The draft** is scripted, standing in for the client's own agent. The platform verifies it; it does not produce it.
- **The agents' runs** are scripted: their outputs are digests of fixed strings, and the walk chooses their self-assessments and the reviewers' verdicts.
- **The one measured outcome** is computed from the prediction itself (its P50 times the ramp factor), so it lands in band by construction.

Every record it writes carries `"demo": true` and a `wrd:demoRecord true` triple. The web UI shows "scripted by the offline demo, not evidence" on every row and calibration cell built on those records.

**The bridge:**

```bash
WRD_DEFAULT_POD_URL=https://pod.example/team/ \
WRD_PLATFORM_KEY=0x<64 hex> \
WRD_RECORD_KEY=<64 hex, a different key> \
WRD_ADMIN_ADDRESSES=0x<admin address> \
npx tsx applications/work-redesign/bridge/server.ts
```

| Variable | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `PORT` | Default `6100`. |
| `HOST` | Default `127.0.0.1`. The bridge holds a signing key, so it never binds every interface unless told to. Bound to loopback, it answers only the Host names `127.0.0.1`, `localhost` and `[::1]` on `PORT` and the host of `BRIDGE_DEPLOYMENT_URL`. Any other Host is refused with 421, so a page that rebinds its own DNS name to 127.0.0.1 gets nothing. Bound to loopback it also trusts no proxy hop for the client address the web UI's sign-in limits count by, and bound to any other address one (see `WRD_TRUST_PROXY_HOPS`). |
| `WRD_TRUST_PROXY_HOPS` | `0` to `4`: how many proxies in front of the bridge set `X-Forwarded-For`, for the client address (`req.ip`) the web UI's sign-in routes limit relay calls by, whatever `HOST` is. Unset: `1` off loopback (the image, behind Railway's edge) and `0` on loopback. A loopback bridge behind a reverse proxy on the same machine sets `1`; left unset there, every caller is the proxy's address, and the per-address limits act as the bridge's own (see **Limits.** under the web UI). A bridge reached directly, with no proxy in front, sets `0`, or a caller can name its own address in `X-Forwarded-For`. Any other value stops the bridge (exit 1, saying why). The startup says which address the limits count by. |
| `BRIDGE_DEPLOYMENT_URL` | The public base URL. Default `http://127.0.0.1:<PORT>`. |
| `WRD_DEFAULT_POD_URL` | The pod used when a call names none. It must be a public http(s) host: the SSRF guard refuses loopback and private addresses. |
| `WRD_SERVED_PODS` | Comma list of the pods records are read from and written to. Default: the default pod. A call naming any other pod is refused (403, `pod-not-served`) before its name is resolved or the pod is read, so the refusal is the same whatever the name resolves to; with neither variable set, every call is refused. Compared after normalisation (scheme and host case, default port, trailing slash). |
| `WRD_POD_WRITE_SECRET` | The operator pod's credential, sent as `Authorization: Bearer` on reads and writes whose URL has the default pod's origin and lies under its path, and nowhere else: not to another origin or path on the same host, and never on a holder copy, which goes out through a separate fetch. Requests that carry it are sent with `redirect: 'manual'`, so the fetch never takes it to a `Location` itself. The SSRF guard re-issues a GET's redirect hop through the same fetch, which decides again for the new URL: a hop that stays under the pod's path and origin carries the credential, and a hop anywhere else does not. A redirected write is not followed. So the credential never leaves the pod's path, redirect or not. The startup says whether one is configured and never prints it. On the shared gate this is the gate's operator token. |
| `WRD_RECORD_KEY` | 64 hex characters (32 bytes), separate from `WRD_PLATFORM_KEY`. Every new record's graph is sealed to the X25519 key derived from it (`deriveEncryptionKeyPair(hex, 'wrd-records')`) and stored as `<slug>-graph.envelope.jose.json`; the bridge opens sealed graphs with it on read. The entity IRIs the bridge derives from who and what a record is about are keyed with a second key derived from it (HMAC-SHA256 under the label `wrd-entity-iri`), and each one names that key's public fingerprint; see "Keyed entity IRIs" for what that hides and what stays public. A malformed value stops the bridge. Every version written with it carries its fingerprint at the end of its record id, so the manifest names the key from its first write. Graphs written before it stay plaintext on the pod (world-readable where the pod serves anonymous reads) until they are removed: the bridge never re-seals a version, and the read model counts them under `plaintext`. Without it records are written in plaintext, and the startup warns that a pod which serves anonymous reads serves them to everyone. **Keep a copy:** a lost or changed key leaves every sealed record unreadable, and a bridge with another key, or none, refuses (409) every write on a pod that holds records written under it, from that key's first write on (see "Keyed entity IRIs"). |
| `WRD_REQUIRE_RECORD_KEY` | `1` makes a missing record key fatal: the bridge exits 1, saying why, unless `WRD_RECORD_KEY` holds a valid key (a blank or whitespace key counts as missing). `0`, or the variable unset, turns the guard off: a local run without it keeps the plaintext default and its warning. Unlike every other variable here, it is read as it is, not with an empty value taken as unset: any other value, a blank or whitespace one included (as an unresolved reference on the service overriding the image's `1` would give), stops the bridge at startup (exit 1, saying the value is blank or neither 1 nor 0) rather than switch the guard off. The bridge's image sets it to `1`, so a deployment cannot fall back to plaintext records. |
| `WRD_PUBLIC_READS` | `1` opens the read model to anyone who can reach the bridge: `wrd.list_records` and `wrd.calibration` answer unsigned GETs, and `wrd.envelope_template` fills its whole template and context from the pod. Otherwise those GETs answer 401, reads go through `wrd.read_records`, `wrd.read_calibration` and `wrd.read_envelope_template` with a signed read in the POST body, and the GET `wrd.envelope_template` fills only an allowlist (see the affordance table). |
| `WRD_PLATFORM_KEY` | The hex secp256k1 key the bridge signs records with, 64 hex characters; a malformed value stops the bridge. Without it the bridge generates an **ephemeral** key and says so at startup. Records written under that key are set aside after a restart, as if never written. |
| `WRD_REQUIRE_PLATFORM_KEY` | `1` makes a missing platform key fatal: the bridge exits 1, saying why, unless `WRD_PLATFORM_KEY` holds a valid key (a blank or whitespace key counts as missing). `0`, or the variable unset, turns the guard off (a local run's default: an ephemeral key, said so). Read as it is, like `WRD_REQUIRE_RECORD_KEY`: any other value, blank or whitespace included, stops the bridge at startup (exit 1, saying why). The bridge's image sets it to `1`: on a host where every deploy is a restart, an ephemeral key would set aside every record at the next deploy, and the registry with them. |
| `WRD_ADMIN_ADDRESSES` | Comma list of the 0x addresses that may change the role registry, compared lower-cased. With none (and no `WRD_ADMIN_DIDS`), every registry change is refused. Any value that is not a 0x address stops the bridge at startup: a user DID goes in `WRD_ADMIN_DIDS`, since read here it would be lower-cased into another user. |
| `WRD_ADMIN_DIDS` | Comma list of Interego user DIDs, `did:web:<WRD_IDENTITY_HOST>:users:<id>`, who are admins too, through a relay-signed request (relay-attested, not user-held). Kept exactly as spelled: never lower-cased, never read as an address. Not baked into the image: the operator sets it on the service. A malformed value, or one set without `WRD_IDENTITY_HOST`, stops the bridge at startup. |
| `WRD_TRUSTED_RELAY_ADDRESSES` | Comma list of the 0x addresses of Interego relay delegation keys this bridge trusts for "Sign in with your Interego account". A request in the relay's signed shape (`_signed_payload`, `_signature`) whose signature recovers to one of them is relay-delegated: the person is the user its agent belongs to. **Relay-attested, not user-held: whoever holds a listed key can act here as any person.** The image sets the live relay's address, `0x276e11d2229d3a96dc96356c15c03c7f83201ebc`; empty (the local default) takes no relay-signed request at all, and any other key signing that shape keeps the key-held route. These are the keys **new** requests are taken from. A malformed address, or one set without `WRD_IDENTITY_HOST`, stops the bridge at startup. |
| `WRD_RETIRED_RELAY_ADDRESSES` | Comma list of the 0x addresses of relay delegation keys retired from new requests. No request one of them signs now is relay-delegated (it takes the key-held route, and is refused there); a countersign one of them signed, stored in a decision, still re-verifies when that decision's outcome is recorded. **To rotate the relay's key:** add the new address to `WRD_TRUSTED_RELAY_ADDRESSES` beside the old one until the relay signs with the new key, then move the old address here, and keep it here while a decision countersigned through it may still have its outcome recorded; dropped from both lists, those outcomes answer 409. A key retired because it may be compromised belongs here too: it takes no new request, and a stored countersign is inside a record the platform signed, so a retired key only re-verifies what this bridge already took from it. Empty in the image. An address in both lists, a malformed one, or one set without `WRD_IDENTITY_HOST` stops the bridge at startup. |
| `WRD_IDENTITY_HOST` | The host the relay's agents and their users are on, as a bare host name; the image sets `identity.interego.xwisee.com`. An agent's DID document is read from `https://<host>/agents/<slug>/did.json` (SSRF-guarded, 8 s, 64 KiB, kept ten minutes), and its `controller` must be `did:web:<host>:users:<id>`. **Whoever controls this host decides whose agent is whose, so can act here as any person.** With it set, the registry accepts user DIDs on it as people. A malformed value stops the bridge. |
| `WRD_RELAY_URL` | The relay a person signs in through, an http(s) base URL; the image sets `https://relay.interego.xwisee.com`. The web UI's "Sign in with your Interego account" goes through it (with a trusted relay address; see **Signing in from the web UI**), and the read model reports it (`bridge.relayDelegation.relayUrl`). A malformed value stops the bridge. |
| `WRD_CHAT_AGENTS_DECIDE` | `1` lets chat agents (Claude, ChatGPT: any agent whose slug does not start `work-redesign-`) take the acts that change who may do what: `wrd.update_registry`, `wrd.countersign_decision` (approving, declining, and approving over an objection or silence), `wrd.record_outcome` (reverted or stopped early, an outcome takes the approved allocation out of force, which for a change within a grant withdraws the agent's authority as `wrd.revoke_grant` does; kept, it settles the decision for good), `wrd.grant_autonomy` and `wrd.revoke_grant`. Unset or `0`, a relay-signed request for one of them is taken only from this bridge's own web UI, whose agent slug starts `work-redesign-`, and is otherwise refused (403, naming this variable). Every other act is open to any relay-delegated principal the bridge's rules allow. Key-held envelopes are not affected. Any other value stops the bridge. |
| `WRD_TRUSTED_PLATFORM_ADDRESSES` | Comma list of earlier platform keys whose records stay trusted, for key rotation. **An address that signed records on the pod stays listed for as long as the pod is used.** Dropped, every graph with a version it signed is cut there: its current version, when that key signed it, is set aside (the graph reads absent, or an earlier version current), and a line that reaches such a version does not read back to its first, so every write to that graph (the registry included) answers 409, naming this variable and the address; the read model lists each such graph under `chainCuts` with `untrustedKey`, and the web UI's header says so. Listing an address only after a later key started chains of its own (an ephemeral key's, say, after a restart) leaves two current versions on every graph both wrote to: those graphs answer 409 (forked), and no affordance resolves a fork. |
| `WRD_CONTACT_URL` | Where the web pages' "Contact us" links go (the landing page, its FAQ and footer, and the guide's contact section), for example a booking page. Unset: the footer's "Contact us" goes to the guide's contact section, `/ui/help#contact`, which tells a visitor to ask the person who sent them the link and links to creating an Interego account; the pricing answer and the closing band then promise no walkthrough, and the band offers the guide instead. **Set it on a service people reach without an introduction**: unset, the pages give a prospect no way to reach you. The bridge says at startup which applies. |
| `WRD_LEGAL_NAME` | The name on the web pages' © line. Unset: `Interego`. |

The bridge serves:

- the affordance manifest at `/affordances`, as Turtle, JSON-LD or HMD;
- MCP at `/mcp`;
- the vocabulary at `/ns/wrd` and `/ns/wrd/shapes`;
- the web UI at `/ui`, also linked from the entry point as "Web UI", with the routes its "Sign in with your Interego account" goes through: `/ui/oauth/client.json`, `/ui/oauth/config`, `/ui/oauth/callback`, `/ui/oauth/exchange`, `/ui/relay/sign` and `/ui/relay/whoami` (see **Signing in from the web UI**). None of them reads or writes a record.

**Web pages.** A browser that opens the bridge's root, `/`, gets the landing page: what Work Redesign is, how it works, who it is for, when not to use it, and a way to sign in. An agent keeps the entry point: the landing page goes only to an `Accept` that names `text/html` (or `application/xhtml+xml`) itself, names no Markdown, and ranks no JSON, JSON-LD or RDF type as high as HTML. So a request with no `Accept` header, `*/*`, JSON-LD, `application/json` ranked above or level with HTML, Turtle, any list that names Markdown, or any `?format=`, gets the JSON-LD or HMD the factory serves, as before, and the answer varies on `Accept`. `/ui` is the app: signed out it shows a welcome screen with the sign-in, signed in a Home page with a checklist for each of the person's roles and what waits for them, then the views. `/ui/help` is the guide, by role, with a glossary. Both new pages are static HTML with no script, under the same Content-Security-Policy as the app, and share one stylesheet, the Interego design system (`applications/_shared/design-system/`) followed by `bridge/ui/app.css`. `bridge/landing.ts` fills them once at start-up from what `server.ts` passes in: the contact link (`WRD_CONTACT_URL`), the legal name (`WRD_LEGAL_NAME`), whether records are sealed (`WRD_RECORD_KEY`: the pages' sentence on encryption says what this server does, and that descriptors keep each record's type, its signers and their roles, its time and its links visible), the relay and identity host the sign-in uses, and the sibling product, Practice Evidence, which every page links to. No email address appears on any page. The app's page is also told whether reads are signed (`WRD_PUBLIC_READS`), so a signed-out visitor's browser asks for no unsigned read it knows the bridge refuses. A signed `wrd.read_records` answer says whether its signer is an admin (`reader.admin`), which is how the app knows to offer People & roles' changes; the bridge checks every change again.

**Sign and verify from a terminal.** Two commands, run from the repository root. The first signs an envelope without a browser wallet; the second verifies a downloaded record without the bridge. Neither needs anything this repository does not already install.

To sign a payload with a local key, take a template from `wrd.read_envelope_template`, POSTing your signed read as `read_envelope` in the JSON body so it is filled from the pod (or from the unsigned GET `wrd.envelope_template`, which fills only its allowlist), fill the paths its `fill` lists, and save it as `payload.json`. A signed read never goes in a URL, because a URL is kept in server, proxy and browser logs and the read opens every record until it is stale: the GET never reads with one, and refuses (400) the forms of one it detects (listed under `wrd.envelope_template` in the affordance table). The key file holds one hex secp256k1 key. The template's `issuedAt` is the bridge's own clock, and its `nonce` is fresh. The command keeps both, and supplies them only when a payload lacks them.

```bash
npx tsx -e "(async () => {
  const { importWallet, signMessageRaw, canonicalJson } = await import('@interego/core');
  const { readFileSync } = await import('node:fs');
  const { randomUUID } = await import('node:crypto');
  const [keyFile, payloadFile] = process.argv.slice(1);
  const key = readFileSync(keyFile, 'utf8').trim();
  const signer = importWallet(key.startsWith('0x') ? key : '0x' + key, 'human', 'signer');
  const p = JSON.parse(readFileSync(payloadFile, 'utf8'));
  const payload = { ...p, issuedAt: p.issuedAt ?? new Date().toISOString(), nonce: p.nonce ?? randomUUID() };
  process.stdout.write(JSON.stringify({ payload, signature: await signMessageRaw(signer, canonicalJson(payload)) }) + '\n');
})()" ./my-key.hex ./payload.json > envelope.json

curl -X POST -H 'content-type: application/json' --data "{\"envelope\": $(cat envelope.json)}" http://127.0.0.1:6100/wrd/update_registry
```

To verify a downloaded record (the file **My record** saves, or a `wrd.holder_copy` response), name the platform address you expect. `wrd.read_records` reports it as `bridge.platform.address`, and the bridge prints it at startup. A download carries each record's graph opened, so it verifies without the record key. Each record's proof must verify, bind the graph bytes in the file, and come from that address. The command exits 1 if any record fails:

```bash
npx tsx -e "(async () => {
  const { readFileSync } = await import('node:fs');
  const { verifyHolderBundle } = await import('./applications/work-redesign/bridge/pod-helpers.ts');
  const [bundleFile, ...platform] = process.argv.slice(1);
  const out = await verifyHolderBundle(JSON.parse(readFileSync(bundleFile, 'utf8')), platform);
  for (const r of out.records) process.stdout.write((r.verified ? 'ok   ' : 'FAIL ') + (r.recordKind ?? '?') + ' ' + (r.status ?? '') + ' ' + r.descriptorUrl + (r.why ? ' — ' + r.why : '') + '\n');
  process.stdout.write((out.ok ? 'verified: ' : 'NOT verified: ') + (out.why ?? out.records.length + ' record(s)') + '\n');
  process.exitCode = out.ok ? 0 : 1;
})()" ./wrd-record.json 0x<platform address>
```

Both were run against the served demo. A registry change signed with the first command was accepted, and the same envelope sent again was refused (409). A 49-record download verified, failed in full against the wrong address, and failed on exactly the record whose graph had one value changed.

**Signing in with an Interego account (relay-attested, not user-held).** A person with no wallet signs in to Interego from Claude, ChatGPT or this bridge's web UI, and acts through the relay's `act` tool with `sign_payload: true`. The relay signs the payload with its delegation key (EIP-191 over `sha256:<hex sha256 of the signed text>`), binding the agent of their session, which they cannot choose, and POSTs `{_signed_payload, _signature}` to the affordance's target. The bridge takes such a request as relay-delegated only when all of this holds (`src/relay-delegated.ts`, then the same checks a key-held envelope gets):

- the signature recovers to an address in `WRD_TRUSTED_RELAY_ADDRESSES` (any other key signing that shape is not relay-delegated: the request goes the key-held route, as it always has);
- `agent_id` is `did:web:<WRD_IDENTITY_HOST>:agents:<slug>`, its DID document there has that id, and its `controller` is `did:web:<WRD_IDENTITY_HOST>:users:<id>`: that user DID is the person. When the agent's id ends in a canonical user id (`<surface>-u-pk-…`, `-u-eth-…`, `-u-did-…`, as the relay mints them), its controller must be that user: the identity host lets any account register an unused agent id, so one it has forgotten after a restart could be claimed by someone else. The id alone never names a person; the agent's own document is always required.;
- the payload's `intent` is the affordance's, `pod_url` names the pod the call targets (the relay's one spelling of the `podUrl` the caller put in the payload; `subject_pod_url`, the caller's own pod, is never read as the pod an act targets), `nonce` is 16 to 64 of `[A-Za-z0-9_-]`, and the relay's `timestamp` is within 15 minutes before and 2 after the bridge's clock (a caller's own `issuedAt` is not read);
- the nonce has not been spent by that person: the in-process ledger and the pod's own record of who authorised each version are kept by principal, never by the relay's address, which signs for everyone. Holder-copy budgets are by principal too: a registered user DID is a member, an unregistered one a stranger.

The person is then that user DID everywhere an address is otherwise: the admin check (`WRD_ADMIN_DIDS`), registry membership and roles (an admin registers a user DID as a person, with a person reference; give a person's key and their user DID the same reference to make them one person for separation of duties), being named on a task or a plan, ratifying, classifying, proposing, approving, grading, reviewing runs, reading and taking a copy. An agent is always a key: it signs its own runs, and a user DID is never registered as one (it may be an agent's accountable principal). A user DID is compared exactly as spelled, so it never collides with an address. The acts that change who may do what (`wrd.update_registry`, `wrd.countersign_decision`, `wrd.record_outcome`, `wrd.grant_autonomy`, `wrd.revoke_grant`) take a relay-delegated request only from this bridge's web UI (an agent slug starting `work-redesign-`) unless the operator sets `WRD_CHAT_AGENTS_DECIDE=1`. That slug comes from the name the OAuth client registered with the relay, so the limit keeps the chat assistants from deciding by default; it is not proof that a request came from this bridge's page.

**What this trusts.** The relay's delegation key can sign for any agent, and the identity host says whose agent it is. So whoever holds that key, or controls the identity host, can act here as any person. A person who signs in this way holds no key, and nothing they sign can be verified without trusting both. Every record a relay-delegated request writes says so: its version's `authorisedBy` carries `authority: "relay-delegated"`, the `agentId` and the `relaySigner`; every envelope it stores carries the same with the `principal` and the exact text the relay signed (`signedPayload`); and the read model shows each head's `authority` (`relay-delegated` or `key-held`), `authorisedBy` and, for a relay-delegated one, its `agentId` and `relaySigner`. Where the bridge re-verifies a stored envelope later (a countersign, when its outcome is recorded), a relay-delegated one is re-checked **offline**: the relay's signature over the stored text must recover to a relay trusted now or retired from new requests (`WRD_RETIRED_RELAY_ADDRESSES`, for a rotated key; a bridge that lists neither answers 409, naming both), the text must name the stored agent on the identity host, the stored principal must be a user there, and the stored payload must be that text as the bridge reads it. The agent's DID document is not fetched again, because it may since have changed or gone: that the agent belonged to that person is what the bridge read from the identity host when it took the act, and a later reader trusts it because the platform's proof covers the record that says so. Holder copies and downloads carry the stored envelopes as they are and verify by the platform's proofs (`verifyHolderBundle`); a reader can re-check a relay-delegated envelope in one the same way, offline, with `verifyStoredEnvelope` in `src/records.ts`.

The relay's `act` POSTs only `{_signed_payload, _signature}`, so whatever else an affordance takes beside the envelope goes inside the payload the relay signs. `wrd.record_task_map` takes its `document_text` there (and `wrd.revise_task` the workflow's document, with new `sourceQuotes`), its sha256 bound as it is beside an envelope. An argument sent beside the pair wins.

**The web UI** (`/ui`) is a plain client over the affordance manifest. It needs no build step and adds no data endpoint of its own: its only routes are the ones a person signs in with their Interego account through, below. A strict Content-Security-Policy applies: no inline script or style, connections to this origin only (`connect-src 'self'`: the page never calls the relay itself), and untrusted text is rendered as text nodes only. It signs in one of four modes, and always shows which one:

1. **Your wallet.** When the browser has an EIP-191 wallet, the page signs with `personal_sign` over `canonicalJson(payload)`. An admin must first add your address to the pod's registry.
2. **Your Interego account.** The "Sign in with your Interego account" button, beside the wallet's, when the bridge has a relay URL and trusts that relay. The relay signs each act for you: relay-attested, not a key you hold (**Signing in from the web UI**, below). The page shows "Signed in as `<your user DID>` via `<agent>`", with a sign-out, and shows your user DID where it would show an address, so you can send it to an admin to be registered.
3. **Demo keys.** Only on the offline demo server. A red banner says the keys are held by that server and these are not signatures by real people.
4. **Neither.** Otherwise, with a sentence saying why. The page then reads only where the bridge serves unsigned reads.

**Signing in from the web UI** (relay-attested, not user-held, and the token passes through the bridge). A person with no wallet presses "Sign in with your Interego account":

1. The page asks the bridge how (`GET /ui/oauth/config`: the bridge reads the relay's `/.well-known/oauth-authorization-server`, and keeps it ten minutes), makes a PKCE verifier and a random state, keeps both in this tab's `sessionStorage`, and sends the tab to the relay's own `/authorize` page: `response_type=code`, `client_id`, `redirect_uri`, `scope=mcp`, `state`, and `code_challenge` (S256 of the verifier), `code_challenge_method=S256`.
2. The person signs in there with their passkey. **What the relay sees:** a client named "Work Redesign", identified by its Client ID Metadata Document, `<BRIDGE_DEPLOYMENT_URL>/ui/oauth/client.json` (`client_id` that URL, `redirect_uris` `[<BRIDGE_DEPLOYMENT_URL>/ui/oauth/callback]`, `grant_types` `authorization_code` only (it asks for no refresh token), `response_types` `code`, `token_endpoint_auth_method` `none`, `scope` `mcp`), which the relay reads itself (SSRF-guarded) and needs nothing registered for. It slugifies the name into the agent of the session, `did:web:<identity host>:agents:work-redesign-<user id>`, whose slug is what the bridge takes decisive acts from (see above). The relay reads such a document only over https and when its metadata says `client_id_metadata_document_supported` (the live relay does); a bridge whose deployment URL is http (a local run), or a relay that does not say so, registers the same metadata once per process by Dynamic Client Registration instead, and `GET /ui/oauth/config` says which (`clientRegistration`).
3. The relay sends the tab back to `/ui/oauth/callback?code=…&state=…`. The page takes both out of the address bar before it sends any request (the manifest's read included). Anyone can send a person to that page, with no state or another and an `error` of their choosing, so the page acts only on the answer that carries the state this tab stored: that answer takes the sign-in flow out of `sessionStorage`, also before any request (so a failed read leaves neither behind), shows the relay's `error` if it carries one, refuses (and exchanges nothing) a sign-in over ten minutes old, and POSTs `{code, code_verifier, state}` to `/ui/oauth/exchange`. An answer with no state or another shows none of its text, says only that this tab did not start this sign-in, exchanges nothing, and leaves the sign-in this tab started waiting for its own answer; that flow goes only once it is over ten minutes old. The bridge exchanges the code at the relay's token endpoint, and that is all it does: it hands the page the relay's OAuth access token for this sign-in and when it ends (`expires_in` from now; the relay's tokens last an hour), and drops the refresh token the relay's answer also carries, so neither the bridge nor the page can renew the sign-in. The page keeps the access token in `sessionStorage` only (this tab, never `localStorage` or a URL), and forgets it when it ends (a timer, and a check before each signature) or on sign-out. The code reaches the bridge once, in the callback's address, as every authorization code does; it is single-use, expires within ten minutes, and is worthless without the verifier only the tab holds.
4. Every signature goes `POST /ui/relay/sign {payload}` with `Authorization: Bearer <that access token>`. The bridge presents the token at the relay's MCP endpoint, `POST <relay>/mcp`, exactly as an MCP client does: one JSON-RPC `tools/call` of `sign_request` with `{payload}` as its arguments and a fresh id, no `initialize` and no session (the relay serves such a call statelessly, and stamps the session's own agent, `did:web:<identity host>:agents:work-redesign-<user id>`, into what it signs). It reads the answer as JSON or as server-sent events, and answers only `{_signature, _signed_payload}`. A JSON-RPC error, a tool error or the tool's own `{error}` is refused (502, the relay's words repeated with the token withheld); a 401 from the relay is refused 401 with `code: "interego-sign-in-expired"` (a 403, a token that cannot sign, 401 with `interego-sign-in-refused`), and the page forgets the token and offers the sign-in again. The bridge presents a plain Bearer, which the relay accepts while it does not require DPoP. The page sends that pair as the act's body (or where an envelope goes: a read's `envelope`, a template's `read_envelope`, a review's `review_envelope`), and the bridge takes it as relay-delegated exactly as it takes one from Claude or ChatGPT. The payload carries no `issuedAt` (the relay's `timestamp` stands for it) and a fresh nonce; the route refuses an intent that is not this bridge's, an agent's run (an agent signs its own runs with its key), and any field the relay would set or drop, so the text the relay signs is the payload sent.
5. The page names you from the first signature: the agent from the text the relay signed (`agent_id`), and the person that agent belongs to from the bridge (`POST /ui/relay/whoami`, the same check the handlers make: a trusted relay's signature, the agent's DID document on the identity host, its user controller), never from anything the page assumes.

**What this trusts, beyond relay delegation itself.** The page holds the relay's OAuth access token for this sign-in, good for about an hour, and it passes through this bridge with every signature. For that hour it lets whoever holds it call the relay's tools as you, as Claude or ChatGPT can with theirs (`sign_request`, and the relay's other tools on your own pod), not only this bridge's acts. It also opens your account: the relay's `GET /identity-token` takes this token and answers with the identity server's bearer behind your sign-in, which is good for up to a day (24 hours from issue) and works at the identity server on the account itself: linking a wallet (`POST /wallet/link`), removing sign-in methods (`DELETE /auth-methods/me/…`) and deleting the account (`POST /users/me/delete`). The bridge never calls `/identity-token` or the relay's REST `/tool/` routes, but that is what its code does, not a boundary, and it cannot ask for a narrower token: `sign_request` needs the scope `mcp` or `mcp:write`, and `/identity-token` takes exactly those. So whoever runs the bridge, and anyone who gets a copy of the access token while it lasts, is trusted with your account for up to a day, past the hour and past signing out. The bridge never logs, stores or echoes the access token, never puts it or the code in a URL it serves, repeats a relay's error text only with the code, the verifier and the token withheld, sends nothing to an endpoint off the relay's origin, follows no redirect, and gives each call to the relay 8 seconds and 256 KiB, within the limits below on how many run a minute and at once. Signing out forgets the token in this tab and revokes nothing: the relay offers no revocation, so the access token stays good at the relay until it ends, and an identity-server token traded for it stays good for its day. "Sign out everywhere" on the identity server's dashboard ends every identity-server token, a traded one included, but not the relay's access token.

**Limits.** Every call the bridge makes to the relay comes from its one address, and the relay limits some routes per client address before it checks any credential: its token endpoint takes 50 requests every 15 minutes (its OAuth router's limit, inside the 60 a minute the relay allows in front of it), its registration endpoint 20 an hour, and it sets no per-address limit on `/mcp`, where each made-up token still costs it a token check. So whoever makes this bridge call the relay spends what everyone signing in here shares, a caller who is not signed in included: made-up codes posted to `/ui/oauth/exchange` would spend the token endpoint's budget, after which no one's sign-in through this bridge finishes until it refills. The bridge therefore admits each relay call before it makes it, and asks the relay nothing past these limits. For callers the relay has not accepted here (a code exchange, the sign-in set-up, which is the relay's metadata and a registration, and a sign with an access token the relay has not accepted from this bridge): at most 20 calls a minute in all, and 10 from one client address; at most 30 code exchanges every 15 minutes in all, and 10 from one address, so the bridge stays well under the token endpoint's 50; at most 10 registrations an hour (one per process when it works); and at most 8 of these calls at once. An access token the relay issued through this bridge counts as a sign-in until it ends, and one the relay answered in a sign counts for an hour. Its calls count only against that sign-in, 20 a minute, and against its person, 40 a minute over all their sign-ins. The person is the user the relay's signed text names, as `POST /ui/relay/whoami` names them (the agent itself when its DID document cannot be read), learned at the first sign with the token; the relay writes that text for the token's own sign-in, so no one can make a token count against someone else. No minute budget a signed-in call counts in is shared with anyone else: the relay keeps no per-address limit on `/mcp` for such a budget to stay under, and whoever holds a token can call the relay's `/mcp` directly at any rate, so a cap over every signed-in call would protect nothing and would let anyone with an Interego account (sign-up is open) stop everyone's signing with a few tokens. What people signed in share is the bridge's own work: at most 64 of their calls run at once, and at most 2 of those for one person. A token the relay refused (401 or 403 at `/mcp`) is refused again from memory for a minute, without a relay call. A limit reached is 429 with `retryAfterSeconds` (and `Retry-After`) and a `code`: `relay-calls-limited` for one caller's own limit (an address not signed in, a sign-in, a person), `relay-busy` for the bridge's, as for a 429 from the relay itself; the page says so when it cannot learn how to sign in. `POST /ui/relay/whoami` calls no relay (it checks the relay's signature itself and reads only the agent's DID document from the identity host, kept ten minutes), so it is not counted; at most 16 run at once.

What remains. Strangers, from enough addresses, can still spend the 20 calls a minute and the 8 places at once for callers not signed in, and the 30 exchanges every 15 minutes, and so delay new sign-ins for as long as they keep it up, and, after a restart (which forgets the tokens the bridge knew), each person's first sign. A person already signed in keeps signing, up to their own limits, whatever strangers and other accounts do, with one exception: the 64 calls at once. One person holds at most 2 of them, so filling them takes 32 Interego accounts, each with a token the relay has accepted here and each keeping 2 calls running. Since each person signs at most 40 times a minute, 32 accounts can do that for about 20 of the relay's answers each minute, a few seconds; keeping it full takes enough accounts that their 40 calls a minute, times how long the relay takes to answer one, add up to 64 running: about 300 accounts at a third of a second an answer. A call refused for it is 429 `relay-busy` with `retryAfterSeconds` 1. Anything else that reaches the relay from the bridge's outbound address shares the relay's per-address budget with it.

The client address is the one the proxy in front of the bridge saw: `server.ts` trusts one proxy hop whenever it does not listen on loopback (the image listens on every interface behind Railway's edge) and none on loopback, and `WRD_TRUST_PROXY_HOPS` sets another number. A loopback bridge behind a reverse proxy on the same machine sees every caller as that proxy unless `WRD_TRUST_PROXY_HOPS=1`: the per-address limits are then the bridge's own, and one stranger at one address can delay every new sign-in, for 15 minutes with 10 made-up codes, or for a minute at a time with 10 made-up tokens, and each refusal tells the person their own address is limited. A bridge listening on a public interface with no proxy in front, with the default left as it is, lets a caller name its own address in `X-Forwarded-For`, so only the bridge's totals hold; `WRD_TRUST_PROXY_HOPS=0` stops that. The counts live in the process. The page does not call the relay itself (its policy is `connect-src 'self'`), so a person cannot spend their own address's budget instead.

Reading is signed too. The page signs one `wrd:read-records` envelope per signer and pod, from the template `wrd.envelope_template` fills, and reuses it for up to nine minutes for every re-read, the calibration view and the "What this signs" templates (sent as `read_envelope` in the POST body of `wrd.read_envelope_template`); the bridge accepts it for fifteen. The page sends a signed read only in a POST body, never in a URL. A reused read the bridge calls stale is signed once more. A signer with no role, while reads are closed, sees the refusal (403) and can switch identity; the page then shows no views, so no "What this signs" either, but it still offers that signer **My record** (copy to a pod, or download): anyone named in a record, or who signed one, takes their copy without a read, so someone removed from the registry still can. With reads opened (`WRD_PUBLIC_READS=1`) the page falls back to the public read model without showing that refusal, and "What this signs" shows the whole template the GET `wrd.envelope_template` fills.

The views are:

- **Tasks.** Ratify, dispute, revise, retire, add and classify tasks, and copy the link for the people named.
- **Roles.** The registry, kinds of task and pod policy.
- **Capture.** By hand, or from a document: a drafting packet for your own agent, the draft pasted into the editable table, and a quote preview in the browser.
- **Plan.** Disabled until every task is classified. Levels are limited by regime and grant, and Emergent tasks get a probe editor.
- **Value.** Ranges with a basis for each input. Unsigned drafts are kept in the browser.
- **Decisions.** Submit, countersign or decline, approve over a silent assignment by naming it, record outcomes, see the version chain, and print a one-decision summary. A chain the bridge could not read back to its first version (`chainUnreadable`) is never shown as whole: the card and the printed summary head it as incomplete and say why beside the heading, a grant's row on **Agents & grants** says the same, and the header counts every chain cut short (the read model's `chainCuts`, every kind of record), and says so separately, naming the address, when a chain reaches a version signed by a platform key the bridge no longer trusts.
- Three signing forms, the classify panel, the Value view and a submitted decision's countersign card, have a "What this signs" button: the payload's JSON Schema and a template filled from the pod, from `wrd.read_envelope_template` (or, with no signed read, the template `wrd.envelope_template` fills: its allowlist while reads are closed, all of it when they are open). The other signing forms have none; the same schema and template for any intent come from those two affordances.
- **Calibration**, **Agents & grants**, and **My record** (copy to your pod, or download).
- A shareable task link, `/ui#task=<iri>`, for the people a task names.

To read another pod, add `?pod=<url>` to the address.

## Records and lifecycle

Each kind of record is one graph. Every change is a new descriptor that supersedes the previous head by its `descriptorUrl`. Every descriptor carries a content-bound authorship proof from the platform key. A version counts as current only if all three hold:

- it sits in `<pod>/wrd/records/`;
- its descriptor holds a proof that verifies against a platform key the bridge trusts;
- that proof's signed record id predicts the URL the descriptor sits at.

Whether a version counts is decided from that signed proof alone, before its graph is read. The proofs the descriptor spells out are tried first: every one it states as RDF, and every `iep:authorshipProof [ … ]` block in its text, comments included and with comments blanked. When none of them places it, the values its text holds are: the bridge signs a record id its URL predicts, the signing key's `did:ethr` as issuer and owner, a content digest, an instant and a signature, and the last three are characters (or `\u` escapes of them) wherever the descriptor holds them, so every combination of them is tried against each trusted key, up to 128, and a signature that recovers to the key it names places the version. The descriptor's subject is not consulted for this: the signature covers the record id, and the URL must be the one that id predicts, so a proof places exactly the URL it was signed for, while the subject line is unsigned bytes. So nothing unsigned in the descriptor decides it: not its spelling (a leading space, a subject joined to its type line, `rdf:type` for `a`, CRLF line ends, the proof moved, spelled with full IRIs or under any prefix name, `\u` escapes in an IRI or a literal, any of Turtle's four quotings), not a comment (one between the subject and its type, one naming `iep:authorshipProof [` or `iep:describes`, a decoy link, even one left open), not an unsigned triple added inside the proof (even one whose literal holds a `]`), not another subject added above it, not a decoy proof block above the real one, such as another version's genuine proof, and not even whether it still parses.

Everything else the bridge reads from a descriptor (the graph it links, the graph it describes, its modal status, the proofs it states, its validity times) is read from its RDF, never from its text: the descriptor is first respelled into one form (comments dropped, `\u` escapes decoded, every prefixed name resolved against its own declarations, any name Turtle admits, every literal in one quoting) and then parsed. So an edit to its spelling or its comments changes nothing the bridge reads. A current version whose graph bytes no longer match its proof is **altered**, and every lifecycle call that needs it answers 409. So is one whose graph is missing, or not found in the document its descriptor links; one whose descriptor, read as RDF, links no graph, more than one, or one outside the pod, no longer describes (`iep:describes`) exactly the graph its proof covers, states a modal status other than the one its signed content records, no longer types the signed record id as an `iep:ContextDescriptor` (its subject changed), or carries an authorship proof besides the one that places it, or that one only in a comment (or none it states correctly, its `iep:signerAddress` changed, say); and one whose descriptor no longer parses at all. The graph it belongs to is still the one its signed content names, or, when its content cannot be read or no longer matches its proof, any graph of the kind its signed record id names that the manifest lists it under. None of these edits sets the version aside, so the version before it never becomes current again in its place, and once the bytes are restored it is trusted again, with no restart and no fork. On an earlier version, the same edits cut the line there (see below), and the respellings and comments above leave it whole. An edit that changes nothing the bridge reads, such as the spelling, comments and unsigned triples above, leaves it trusted.

Edits that make a current version ineligible, or leave it out of its graph's listing, are different, and they are the only edits that act as a deletion would. Deleting its descriptor, editing or removing its proof so that no signed value of it is left in the descriptor's text (or so that no proof the descriptor holds verifies under a trusted key for the URL it sits at, its record id or signature changed, say), filling a descriptor whose proof it no longer spells out with so many signatures, instants and digests that its own are not among the 128 combinations tried, or removing its manifest entry (or changing the entry's `dct:conformsTo` so it names no `wrd:` kind, which does the same), listing it under another graph or changing the URL the entry names, sets it aside, so the version before it becomes current again: writes proceed from that version (while it is fresh, a replay of the envelope that wrote the version set aside among them), and once the bytes are restored the graph is forked (409). So does removing from `WRD_TRUSTED_PLATFORM_ADDRESSES` the address that signed it (see that variable). Nothing signed says which version of a graph is current, so the bridge cannot tell such an edit from a version that was never written; v1 relies on the pod's access control here (see "Deviations and honest limits"). Supersedes links in the manifest alone never make an older version current, nor a graph absent: a link from an entry to itself is ignored, a current version whose link to the version before it is pointed elsewhere leaves both current (forked, 409), and links that leave no version current, a cycle, make the graph answer 409 (forked), never absent. A graph's whole chain is never read short, whatever the edit: every version carries its number in its signed content, so a read that needs the whole chain (the replay check, a retirement's start, a holder's copy, a decision's `chain`, a grant's `grantedAt`) refuses (409) or says so under `chainUnreadable` when the walk back from the current version does not reach its first, because an earlier version is not trusted (its graph changed, missing or not opening, its descriptor altered as above), was signed by a platform key the bridge no longer trusts (the refusal names `WRD_TRUSTED_PLATFORM_ADDRESSES` and the address), or is missing (deleted, its proof broken, its manifest entry removed or changed). The read model lists every graph whose line is cut, whatever its kind, under `chainCuts`, with why (and `untrustedKey` for the second). A holder's copy is refused (409) as well while any graph's current version is altered or forked: it cannot tell whether that record names the holder.

**Sealed graphs.** With a record key, each version's graph is sealed to it before it is written; the pod holds `<slug>-graph.envelope.jose.json`, and the descriptor links it as an encrypted distribution. The proof covers the plaintext graph, so the checks above run on the opened bytes. A sealed graph that does not open (sealed to another key, no key held, or its envelope changed on the pod: its ciphertext or its recipients) is never read as a record, and never as absent or negative: if its descriptor is otherwise current, the graph is **altered** and answers 409 naming `WRD_RECORD_KEY`, so an older version never becomes current again in its place; a grant that does not open is neither active nor inactive, an acknowledgement that does not open is not "not acknowledged", and a holder's copy is refused rather than returned without it, whether it is a current version or an earlier one in a chain (a chain walked back to a version that does not open is cut short there, so every version before it would be missing too). A write that reads a graph's whole chain, such as the replay check or a retirement's start, refuses with the same 409. The read model never answers from such a record's absence: a task whose workflow does not open has `orphaned` and `retirementUnfinished` null and says why under `workflowUnreadable`; a plan proposed against a workflow, task or classification that does not open has `stale` null (unless a record that does open already shows it stale) and says why under `unreadable`; a task's allocation in force is marked `unreadable` when it may come from a decision, or from the plan of an approved decision, that does not open, and then whether it is one the task's classification allows is unknown too: `currentNotPermitted` is null and `currentNotPermittedUnreadable` says why (as it does when the classification does not open), never worked out from the captured allocation as if the decision were absent, and the read model lists it under `unreadable.currentNotPermitted`; and a decision's `chain` or a grant's `grantedAt` read from a chain cut short says so under `chainUnreadable`. A figure counted from records of a kind never counts one that does not open as absent: the kind is public in every IRI, so when a run or a decision does not open, every figure that could depend on it says so under `unreadable`, naming why (`WRD_RECORD_KEY` when it is sealed): each task's `contraryEvidence` (its counts are of the runs that open, and `suggestReclassify` is null unless a critical failure among them settles it), each row of `runs`, the read model's `unreadable.runs`, `unreadable.awaitingReview` and `unreadable.contraryEvidence`, and in the calibration each prediction cell, agent cell and `probeSignals`, with `unreadable.predictionCells`, `unreadable.probeSignals`, `unreadable.agentCells` and `unreadable.awaitingAPerson`. Which run or decision it is, which task, agent or prediction cell it is about, is not public, so the mark is on every figure of its section. The read model lists all of these under `sealed`.

**Keyed entity IRIs.** Each graph's IRI is derived from a tuple: a plan and a task and the person assigned to it, an agent and a kind of task, a plan and "probe", a workflow and a task's index. Without a record key it is `urn:wrd:<kind>:<scope>:<id>`, two 16-hex sha256 segments. The manifest names every graph, so anyone who can guess a tuple recovers it from such a hash. With a record key it is `urn:wrd:<kind>:k<fingerprint>:<scope>:<id>`: each segment is the first 16 hex of HMAC-SHA256 of `kind \0 position \0 seed` (position `scope` or `id`) under a key derived from `WRD_RECORD_KEY` (label `wrd-entity-iri`), so only a holder of the key recovers a tuple, and one seed gives unrelated segments in different kinds and positions (a grant's scope is not the holder copy's of the same agent, a task's scope is not a plan's of the same workflow). The fingerprint is the first 8 hex of HMAC-SHA256 of `wrd-iri-key-fingerprint` under that derived key: it identifies which key an IRI was derived under and reveals nothing about the key.

Records written before the key keep their unkeyed IRIs: every lookup tries the keyed form and then the unkeyed one, and a chain that exists under its unkeyed IRI keeps growing there. Their graphs stay plaintext. The bridge never re-seals a version (its proof binds the bytes and the URL it was written at), so on a pod that serves anonymous reads, as the gate does, anyone can read them until they are removed from the pod; with a key, the read model counts them under `plaintext` (with the first 20 descriptor URLs) and `readExposure` says how many there are. A bridge holding another key, or none, derives other IRIs for the same tuples, so it would find those records absent and start second chains beside them; it cannot open what was sealed under the key; and what it writes is either sealed where the key's own bridge cannot open it (a registry sealed so would shut that bridge out) or, with no key, plaintext. So every version written with a record key says so in its record id, `urn:wrd:record:<kind>-<16 hex>-k<fingerprint>`, under the same fingerprint as the key's IRIs. The platform's proof signs the id, and the descriptor sits at the URL predicted from it, so neither can be changed without the version being set aside: from the key's first write, a new version of a chain written before the key included, the manifest names the key, before any IRI is keyed. When the pod holds a version a trusted platform key signed whose record id names another key (or any key, when this bridge holds none), or whose signed content names an IRI keyed under another key, the bridge refuses every write and every lookup by tuple with 409 naming `WRD_RECORD_KEY` and both fingerprints, whoever signs it: the check runs before the registry is read. When the bridge cannot use the pod's registry either, as when the key's own bridge wrote the registry first (the usual go-live order, which seals it to that key), it answers every read with the same 409, saying why the registry is shut: `wrd.read_records`, `wrd.read_calibration` and `wrd.read_envelope_template`, and, when reads are opened, `wrd.list_records`, `wrd.calibration` and `wrd.envelope_template`, whether an admin, a registered key or anyone else signs, since without the registry it can neither tell who may read nor build a read model; the web UI shows that refusal. Otherwise its read model says so under `recordKeyMismatch` and marks every answer that rests on a record written under the other key: a task whose classification it cannot find is `unreadable`, not unclassified, an assignment's acknowledgement is `unreadable`, a plan's staleness is unknown, and a grant is neither active nor inactive. Only the manifest entries whose descriptor URL names another key, or that name a graph keyed under one, are read for this, once per call: on a pod written throughout under this bridge's key, or under none, the check reads nothing. A version the pod cannot serve therefore stops no write, unless it is one of those: then the bridge cannot tell, and refuses every write and every lookup by tuple (502, naming the version) until the pod serves it. Which key a version was written under is never taken from its envelope's recipient list or its descriptor's `iep:describes`, which a party able to write the pod can change: such an edit to a version of this bridge's own key makes that graph answer 409 (altered) where it is used (a current version on every call that needs it, an earlier one wherever the graph's whole chain is read) and stops nothing else, and once the bytes are restored the graph reads again, with no restart. A manifest entry a pod writer adds, naming a keyed graph or at a URL naming a key, with no version a trusted key signed, changes nothing. The registry's IRI is derived from the pod URL alone and is never keyed, so every bridge on the pod finds the same one, and one holding another key, or none, refuses (409) instead of starting a second registry.

What keyed IRIs still show is the key's fingerprint, and which records share a segment. Only records of one kind can share one, and only at the same position:

- **Scope.** Records of one kind about one thing share their scope segment, because the bridge selects them from the manifest by it: tasks, classifications, plans and decisions of one workflow; valuations and acknowledgements of one plan; runs of one agent on one kind of task; grants of one agent; holder copies of one holder; all workflows on the pod. Several of these links are public anyway through the descriptors (a plan is derived from its workflow's version, a valuation and an acknowledgement from their plan's, a decision from its plan's and valuation's, a classification and a run from their task's, a holder copy is signed by its holder); the scope adds which runs share an agent and kind of task and which grants share an agent, and which tasks share a workflow, which times already show (below).
- **Id.** Records of one kind with the same id share their id segment: a task's position across workflows, one person's acknowledgements of one task across plans, grants on one kind of task across agents, valuations of the same inputs across plans, runs given the same run id.

No segment is shared between two kinds or two positions.

**Times link what one call writes.** Every record a call writes carries the call's instant: they share `generatedAtTime` and `validFrom`, and they are written one after another in the call's order. Only three calls write records of two kinds: `wrd.capture_workflow` and `wrd.record_task_map` write a workflow's first version with its tasks, and `wrd.revise_task` writes an added or retired task with the workflow version that lists or drops it. So which workflow a task belongs to is public from the descriptors alone, whatever its IRI hides, and so is the order a capture wrote its tasks in, which is their order in the workflow, wherever the order of writes shows (the manifest's order, the pod's modification times). Every other call writes one record.

**What stays public with a record key.** The descriptors and the manifest: each record's kind, its signers and whether each signed as curator or validator (and is a software agent), its modal status and confidence, its validity times and when it was generated, the version it supersedes and the versions it was derived from, its graph digest, the fingerprint of the key it was written under (its record id and descriptor URL end with it) and of the key its IRI was derived under, and the segments it shares with records of its kind (see "Keyed entity IRIs"). What those show together stays public too. A task version signed by a validator alone and left Hypothetical is a dispute by one party; an acknowledgement's signer, Asserted, derived from a plan, is a person named in that plan; a decision that carries a confidence and derives from a valuation is a valued one, and one that does neither is a probe; derivation links tie a classification to its task's version and a run to its task's version; and the records one call writes share their times, which ties each task to its workflow (see "Times link what one call writes"). The graphs (names, descriptions, answers, valuations, notes, addresses inside payloads) and the tuples behind the IRIs do not, except a task's: which workflow it belongs to, and its place among the tasks captured with it, are public through those times and the order of the writes.

**The manifest is a hint.** Which descriptors describe a graph, and which version supersedes which, are read from the manifest, which anyone able to write the pod can edit. A version whose content its proof binds counts toward a graph only when that signed content names the graph, and a supersedes link is followed only to a trusted version of the same graph, so a link added from one chain into another entity's never puts that record (which the bridge could open) into a chain, a read or a holder's copy. A version a trusted key signed where its proof says, whose content cannot be read or no longer matches its proof, counts, never as trusted, toward any graph of the kind its signed record id names that the manifest lists it under: such a listing, or a link to it from a chain of that kind, can make that graph answer 409 (the refusal names that version's descriptor), and never puts anything in a read. A supersedes link from an entry to itself is ignored, and links that leave no version current make the graph answer 409, never absent. What the manifest cannot be made to do is keep a current version that is left out of it: removing a current version's entry, listing it under another graph or changing its URL makes the version before it current, as deleting it would (see above).

```
Task            captured[H] ──every party ratifies──▶ ratified[A];  any dispute ──▶ disputed[H] ──revise──▶ captured[H];  retire ──▶ retired[Retracted]
                (a revision that removes someone named owes them a ratification: it stays captured until they ratify, and they can dispute it)
Classification  classified[H] ──every party ratifies──▶ ratified[A];  dispute (with own answers) ──▶ disputed[H] ──classify again──▶ …
Plan            proposed[H] (valued | probe; bound to the classifications and task texts it was checked against)
Valuation       valued[H] (same plan + inputs ⇒ same record)
Decision        submitted[H] ──approve──▶ countersigned[A, "approved; prediction unmeasured", in force] ──outcome──▶ measured[A]
                          └──decline──▶ declined[Counterfactual, prediction not to be measured]
AgentRun        reviewed[A];  or acted-under-grant[H] ──a person reviews later──▶ reviewed[A]
Grant           granted[A] ──revoke──▶ revoked[Retracted, valid from the grant to the revocation; work under it suspended] ──re-earned──▶ granted[A]
```

`[H]` is Hypothetical, `[A]` is Asserted. The modal status a descriptor carries must match the status in its signed payload.

**Envelope.** An envelope has the form `{payload, signature}`. The signature is EIP-191 `personal_sign` over `canonicalJson(payload)`. Every payload carries:

- `intent`;
- `podUrl`, which must equal the pod the call targets;
- `issuedAt`, accepted from 15 minutes before to 2 minutes after the bridge's clock;
- a `nonce`, refused if reused (see **Integrity and replay** below). A signed read (`wrd:read-records`) changes nothing and spends no nonce: the same envelope reads again until its `issuedAt` leaves the window.

Identity is the address recovered from the signature, or, for a relay-signed request (`_signed_payload` and `_signature` in place of the envelope, from a relay this bridge trusts), the user DID the identity host says the relay's agent belongs to, with the pod read from `pod_url` and the time from the relay's `timestamp` (see **Signing in with an Interego account**). No affordance takes a caller DID, key or seed. Payloads that change an existing record also bind the head they read, and are refused with 409 if it has moved on:

| Payloads | Bind |
|---|---|
| classify | the task description's `taskTextSha256` |
| ratify, revise | the head's `descriptorUrl` and `graphDigest` |
| countersign, outcome, revoke | the head's `recordId`, `descriptorUrl` and `graphDigest` |

**Payload shapes.** Each envelope affordance's description in the manifest summarises its payload in one line. `wrd.envelope_template` returns the full JSON Schema (draft 2020-12) and a template, and `wrd.read_envelope_template`, for a signed read, the same template filled from the pod. A malformed payload is answered 400 with that schema as `expectedPayload`.

**Quote rule.** Normalise both the document and the quote: Unicode NFC, curly quotes made straight, any whitespace run collapsed to one space, then trimmed. Matching is case-sensitive, and a quote needs at least 12 characters. Offsets index the normalised document. A quote that does not match is `unverified` with a reason, and is never repaired.

**Valuation.** Each run makes 5000 independent triangular draws, which the record states. The result gives P10, P50 and P90 for the weekly change and for the NPV over the horizon (linear ramp, monthly discount), plus P(NPV > 0) and the payback month, for both figures. It also reports:

- freed person-hours per task and in total, with each task's disposition (redeployed, absorbed, unfilled, undecided);
- a cost breakdown at likely values: value, expected loss, person cost, oversight cost, agent cost;
- the value-to-cost ratio as a band;
- a one-at-a-time sensitivity ranking, with each input's basis;
- inputs counted by basis, single-number inputs flagged "no range given", and estimated agent costs set beside the costs agents reported on person-reviewed runs, labelled "agent-reported";
- pilot size.

The inputs it refuses (400, nothing recorded):

- any value above 10^12, and any inputs whose result is not a finite number;
- `measured` on a proposed state without a `source`: the proposed state has not run yet;
- `not-applicable` that is not 0 / 0 / 0, or outside a task's current or proposed state;
- zero oversight hours on a proposed state in which a person decides on or audits an agent's work.

Its stated assumptions:

- Inputs are independent; correlation is not modelled.
- Value per unit is unchanged by the change.
- The ramp is linear.
- Failure costs are expected costs: rate times cost per failure.
- Oversight is costed at the task's hourly cost.
- Freed hours count as cash only where the work stays unfilled.
- Estimates are unmeasured.

Percentiles do not add, so the UI never totals P50s across decisions.

**Outcome grading.** The measurement window must start at or after the countersign and must have ended. It must also be as long as the `windowWeeks` the decision declared: a shorter window is refused for a kept change, and for a reverted or stopped-early change is recorded as `windowShorterThanPlanned` and left out of calibration. The predicted band is scaled by the mean ramp factor over the window. With a measured interval, a result that straddles a band edge is inconclusive: it is excluded from calibration and counted. Every outcome records its disposition: kept, reverted or stopped-early. The pilot check counts the person-reviewed runs on the plan's agent-level tasks inside the window, against the pilot size.

**What is in force.** An approved valued decision is in force from its countersign, while it is measured. A kept outcome keeps it in force; a reverted or stopped-early outcome returns its tasks to their earlier allocation. Only one approved, unmeasured decision may change a task at a time: a second is refused at submission and at approval until the first has an outcome.

**Calibration.**

- Prediction cells are kept per metric × decision level × comparison class. They show coverage against the nominal 0.8, Brier score and mean level distance, and stay Hypothetical below 20 samples.
- Agent-run cells are kept per agent × kind of task × model.
- Probe signals are reported apart: a probe predicts nothing.

**Grant policy.** The defaults are published in `wrd.ttl` (`wrd:defaultGrantPolicy`):

- 20 person-reviewed runs on the declared model, of which a person accepted at least 90% (`wrd:floor`, applied by the judgment kit's earned-autonomy rule to acceptance outcomes);
- at least 5 person confirmations from at least 5 distinct registered reviewers the admin attests are different people. **This bridge cannot verify that they are different people.**
- at least 20 runs the agent called ready, of which a person accepted at least 90%, with no critical failure among them;
- for critical work, ⌈3 / 0.01⌉ = 300 clean runs.

The calibration view's agent cells show both the share a person accepted and how often the agent's own label matched the reviewer's (`hitRate`).

A pod policy may only tighten these.

## Projections

None are built in v1. An xAPI statement per agent run or decision event would lose:

- the modal status (Hypothetical, Asserted, Counterfactual, Retracted);
- the supersedes chain;
- quote offsets and verdicts;
- the input bases;
- the signed envelopes.

A task may cite an external task term (`externalTaskTerm`, for example an O\*NET task statement, CC BY 4.0) beside its local kind of task.

## Deviations and honest limits

**People and signatures**

- **People are told apart by an admin attestation, not by the bridge.** Separation of duties and "distinct reviewers" compare the person reference an admin declares for each key. One person may hold several keys. The bridge cannot verify that two person references are two people, and every report says so.
- **Signing in from the web UI passes the person's access token through this bridge, and with it their account.** The relay's OAuth access token for their sign-in (about an hour) goes through the bridge with every signature. For that hour it lets its holder call the relay's tools as the person, not only this bridge's acts, and the relay's `GET /identity-token` trades it for the identity server's bearer behind the sign-in, good on the account itself (linking a wallet, removing sign-in methods, deleting the account) for up to a day. The bridge never asks for that bearer, but no narrower token can sign, so its operator, and anyone with a copy of the access token while it lasts, is trusted with the person's account for up to a day. Signing out forgets the access token in the tab and revokes nothing; it stays good at the relay until it ends, and a traded identity-server token for its day. Every relay call comes from the bridge's one address, so strangers, from enough addresses, can still delay new sign-ins through it while they keep at it (see **Limits.** under the web UI). They cannot stop the signing of anyone already signed in, and nor can anyone with an Interego account, since each person's signed-in calls count only in budgets of their own; what people signed in share is 64 calls at once, 2 per person, which 32 accounts signing at the same moment fill for a few seconds a minute. A loopback bridge behind a reverse proxy on the same machine sees every caller as that proxy unless `WRD_TRUST_PROXY_HOPS=1`, and then one stranger can delay every new sign-in.
- **Workers need an EIP-191 wallet, or an Interego account** to ratify, dispute, acknowledge or take a copy. Signing in with an Interego account is relay-attested, not user-held: the relay signs for them, so whoever holds its delegation key or controls the identity host can act as them, and their records say `relay-delegated`. Passkey and did:key signing are not built. There is deliberately no unsigned "claim this task by link".
- **The limit on decisive acts rests on a name the client chose.** A relay-delegated registry change, countersign, outcome, grant or revocation is taken only when its agent's slug starts `work-redesign-` (unless `WRD_CHAT_AGENTS_DECIDE=1`). The relay derives that slug from the name the OAuth client registered with, so it keeps Claude and ChatGPT from deciding by default, and does not prove the request came from this bridge's page.
- **Expert evidence for Knowable work is who answered, not a label on an input.** It is classification answers from an approver or from a person named on the task, or a classification everyone named on it ratified. A classifier named on the task answers as `performer` (a person) or `agent`; one who is not, as `approver` if they hold that role. In the demo, Ana is an operator and an approver who is not named on the tasks she classifies, so her classifications record `answeredBy.role: approver`.
- **Separation of duties compares person references.** Whoever proposed, valued or submitted a decision, the people it changes, and the principals of agents it names cannot approve it. Whoever prepared or approved it cannot grade it.

**Access and storage**

- **Reads through the bridge are scoped to signers it knows.** `wrd.read_records` and `wrd.read_calibration` answer a signed read from an admin or a key in the pod's registry, and refuse (403) any other key. Membership is decided on the whole manifest: when part of it does not answer, a signed read by any key but an admin's is refused (502) as a write is, so a key removed by a registry version the bridge cannot reach is not let back in by the version before it. The unsigned GETs `wrd.list_records` and `wrd.calibration` answer 401 unless the operator sets `WRD_PUBLIC_READS=1`, and until then the GET `wrd.envelope_template` fills only its allowlist. Every registered key reads every record on the pod, including dispute notes, valuations and addresses: the scope is the registry, not the record. A signed read is reusable until it is stale (15 minutes), so whoever holds a copy of one can read for that long; it is therefore taken only in a POST body. The one GET that could carry it (`wrd.envelope_template`) never reads with it, and refuses (400) the forms of one it detects (see the affordance table), so a client that put one in a URL is told to sign a new one; a form it does not detect still is not read. The UI says which applies on every screen. The integrity refusals of `wrd.envelope_template` and of signed writes still name record IRIs and states.
- **Encryption at rest covers graphs, not descriptors, and only the graphs written with the key.** With `WRD_RECORD_KEY`, every record graph the bridge writes is sealed on the pod and the entity IRIs it derives are keyed; graphs written before the key stay plaintext, readable by anyone on a pod that serves anonymous reads until they are removed (the read model counts them under `plaintext`, and `readExposure` says how many); the descriptors and the manifest stay public (signers and their roles, kind, modal status and confidence, times, supersedes and derivation links), and so does what those show together, such as a task version signed by a validator alone and left Hypothetical being one party's dispute (see "What stays public with a record key"). Without it, records are plaintext, and on a pod that serves anonymous reads, as the shared gate does, anyone can read every one. The bridge holds the only key: there is no second recipient and no key rotation, so keep a copy of the key. The registry is a record like any other, sealed to the same key, which is why a bridge with another key or none refuses (409) rather than act on a registry it cannot read; and on a pod holding records written under another key (a version whose record id names it, or an IRI keyed under it) it refuses every write, even where the registry is still plaintext (written before the key), so it never seals that registry where the key's own bridge cannot read it. A lost key therefore stops every write to the pod's records: the operator restores the key, or moves to a new pod.
- **`HOST` defaults to loopback, and document text is stored only when the operator opts in.** On loopback, and always in the demo, a request whose Host header is not one of the bridge's own names is refused (421) before any route except the shared `/health`, which returns only the status and build. A bridge bound to another interface does not check Host: put it behind a proxy that does.
- **The operator's pod credential, when configured, goes to the operator's pod only.** `WRD_POD_WRITE_SECRET` is added to requests under `WRD_DEFAULT_POD_URL`'s path and origin and never to another pod or path. A GET redirect hop the SSRF guard re-issues carries it again only when the new URL is still under the pod's path; the fetch itself never follows a redirect with it. Without it, use a pod or container that accepts the bridge's writes. Pods on loopback or private addresses are refused by the SSRF guard. Holder copies go out without the credential, so they need a pod that accepts them; otherwise use the download. Copies are plaintext even when the originals are sealed: encrypted copies are out of scope.

**Integrity and replay**

- **Replay protection reads the pod.** Every record version stores the signer and nonce of the envelope that authorised it, and a write is refused (409) when the graph's chain already holds that pair, so it holds after a restart. Writes that start a graph mint an IRI from the nonce, or one unique to their subject, so a replay finds the graph already there. An in-process ledger, bounded to 100 000 entries and claimed only for registered keys and admins, refuses repeats before any read. Supersede is serialised per graph within one process; two bridge processes on one pod can still race between read and write.
- **Adding or retiring a task writes two records: the task, then its workflow.** If the workflow write fails, the answer says what was written and what to call next.
  - **A failed retirement** leaves a retired task that the workflow still lists; the read model (`wrd.read_records`) marks it `retirementUnfinished`. Until it is finished, any proposal on that workflow answers 409, and so does valuing, submitting or approving a plan that changes the task. The 409 names the fix: retire the task again. Any operator can send that call, bound to the retired version or to the version it retired. It writes only the workflow version that drops the task, records which retirement it finishes in `revision.repairs`, and answers `repaired: true`. The envelope that retired the task cannot be replayed to finish it.
  - **A failed add** leaves an `orphaned` task, which no plan can name (`orphaned` is null, not true, while the task's workflow does not open). Add the task again, and retire the orphan to clear it.
- **A party with write access to the pod can still delete records, or overwrite a genuine descriptor in place.** Descriptors that are unsigned, foreign, replayed to another URL, or holder copies are all ignored, and so is a manifest entry or supersedes link that puts a version whose signed content names one entity's graph under another's. The exception is a version a trusted key signed where its proof says whose content cannot be read or no longer matches its proof: it counts, never as trusted, toward any graph of the kind its signed record id names that the manifest lists it under, so such a listing, or a link to it, can make that graph answer 409 (naming that version); it never puts anything in a read. Edits that change a record's bytes, or what its descriptor states as RDF about its modal status, its `iep:describes`, its link to its graph, its subject or the proofs it carries, or that leave it unparseable, are detected and answered 409, and so are supersedes links that leave two versions current or none; an edit to its spelling or comments changes nothing, because everything the bridge reads from a descriptor is read as RDF and its proof is found by its signed values, however they are spelled. Other facet edits are not covered by the platform's proof. Only deleting a current version, removing its proof's signed values from the descriptor (or breaking them so that none verifies under a trusted key for the URL it sits at), or removing, moving or re-addressing its manifest entry makes the version before it current: writes proceed from that version, and once the bytes are restored the graph is forked (409). A read of a whole chain is never answered short by any of these: it refuses (409), or the read model says so under `chainUnreadable` and `chainCuts`.
- **An ephemeral platform key makes earlier records untrusted after a restart.** Set `WRD_PLATFORM_KEY`; the image requires it (`WRD_REQUIRE_PLATFORM_KEY=1`) and does not start without it. A bridge with a new key starts new chains, and the registry records `replacedUntrusted: true`. Trusting the old key's address afterwards does not join them: every graph both keys wrote to then has two current versions, and answers 409 (forked) to every call, the registry's included, with no affordance that resolves a fork.
- **A rotated-out platform address stays trusted for as long as the pod is used.** Nothing signed says a key was retired, so the bridge cannot tell a version signed by a key it no longer trusts from one forged with it: dropping the address from `WRD_TRUSTED_PLATFORM_ADDRESSES` cuts every line holding a version it signed, and every write to those graphs answers 409 until it is listed again. A leaked platform key therefore cannot be distrusted without freezing what it signed; the way out is a new pod.
- **A holder's copy reads the whole pod.** Anyone named in a record or signing one may take a copy, registered or not, so the bridge cannot tell such a holder from a key made for the purpose until it has read every record. It bounds that instead: a holder copy from a key neither an admin nor in the registry spends its nonce and draws on one budget all such keys share (4 a minute, charged when a call arrives), in a lane of their own that takes one copy at a time, while admins and registry members have their own 2 copies at once; beyond any of these it answers 429 before reading any record. A determined caller can still keep the strangers' budget spent, delaying a removed member's copy by a minute at a time, but can no longer block an admin's or a member's copy.

**What the platform records**

- **Measured outcomes and probe signals are entered and signed by a person, not instrumented.** Agent outputs are referenced by digest only.
- **Agent run costs are what the agent reports.** They are counted only on runs a person reviewed, and are labelled "agent-reported on person-reviewed runs". Nothing meters them.
- **Freed-hours dispositions live on the valuation inputs**, because the cash figure is computed from them. The decision binds the valuation hash and shows them.
- **Probe decisions bind no valuation and carry no prediction.** They are still submitted, countersigned under the same separation rules, and read as amplify, dampen or neither.
- **A disputed classification blocks changes to that task only**, not plans that leave it unchanged.
- **Kinds of task are declared by an admin in the registry**, like every other registry change.

**Interface and demo**

- **Affordances beyond the original list.** `wrd.log_agent_run` exists because a grant has to be earned from real reviewed runs. `wrd.revise_task` exists because a dispute needs a way to be answered. `wrd.envelope_template` exists because a Path-A agent must be able to discover what to sign. `wrd.read_records`, `wrd.read_calibration` and `wrd.read_envelope_template` exist because a bridge that opens sealed records must not hand them to anyone who asks, and must not take the read that opens them in a URL. `wrd.demo_envelope` and `wrd.demo_advance_clock` are demo only. The registry affordance is `wrd.update_registry`.
- **Every lifecycle path is exercised against the in-memory pod, not a real Solid server.** The production composition (`bridge/server.ts`) has been started, and its health, manifest and UI read. A real deployment needs a pod that accepts the bridge's writes (with `WRD_POD_WRITE_SECRET` where the pod requires a credential), a stable `WRD_PLATFORM_KEY` and `WRD_RECORD_KEY`, and admin addresses. The admin can sign with the terminal command above instead of a browser wallet.
- **The bridge's answers carry more than the minimum.** The read model (`wrd.read_records`, or `wrd.list_records` when reads are opened) also returns `records`, `integrity` (per altered or forked graph, its heads and why), `sealed` (graphs whose current version, or an earlier version this answer walked back to, does not open), `orphaned`, `retirementUnfinished` and `workflowUnreadable` on tasks, `unreadable` on plans whose staleness cannot be read, `chainUnreadable` on decisions and grants whose chain is cut short, `chainCuts` (every graph, of any kind, whose line does not read back to its first version, with why, and `untrustedKey` when the version it stops at was signed by a platform key the bridge no longer trusts), `unreadable` on figures counted from runs or decisions that do not all open (and on the read model and the calibration, per section), `recordKeyMismatch` when the pod holds records written under another record key (a version whose record id names it, or an IRI keyed under it: from that key's first write, before any IRI is keyed), `plaintext` when a bridge holding a key finds versions written before it, and each workflow's latest `revision`. Unexpected failures answer 500, not the shared bridge's default 400, and so does a record the bridge cannot seal: sealing happens in the bridge, so it is never reported as the pod's failure.
- **Text must be well-formed Unicode.** A string with a lone UTF-16 surrogate, anywhere in a call's arguments however deeply nested (the check has no depth past which it stops looking), is refused (400) before any work: it cannot be signed, hashed or sealed as it was sent.
- **The document-capture quote preview runs in the browser**, by a port of the published rule. The platform's check when you sign is the one that counts.
- **In the demo UI, a run logged from the page states `performedAt` as the newest record time on the pod.** That is the demo server's scripted clock, not the browser's. In wallet mode the run envelope is signed by the agent's own key and pasted in.
- **The wallet signing path in the UI was not exercised against a real wallet extension in this build.** The demo-key path was driven in headless Chromium: every view, a signed registry change, a refusal shown in the page, the task link and a record download. The canonical-JSON port follows `@interego/core`'s algorithm; the bridge verifies whatever arrives.

## What v1 does not do

- No text generation of any kind. Drafting is the client's own agent, through the packet.
- No HRIS or LMS import.
- No LRS projection.
- No cross-organisation aggregates.
- No encrypted copies, and no record-key rotation or second recipient.
- No read scoping finer than the registry: every registered key reads every record.
- No passkey or did:key signing. Signing in with an Interego account is relay-attested (the relay signs), not a key the person holds.
- No interviews. Capture is by hand or from a document's text; the UI loads `.txt` or `.md` files, and nothing extracts text from PDF, Word or spreadsheets.
- No generated options. People propose allocations; the platform checks, values and records them.
- No withdrawing a submitted decision. An approver declines it, and the operator proposes a new plan.
- No correlated-failure stress test. Inputs are drawn independently.
