Getting a second person in

This page reads and writes through the connector of whoever is looking at it, so it cannot be handed round as a link — a second person publishes their own copy of the same file and it becomes their page, on their pod, under their credentials.

  1. They add the Interego connector in claude.ai, under Settings → Connectors.
  2. They publish this file from their own account, granting it the eleven tools listed in Publishing this page below. The file itself needs no edit; the tool list is an argument passed at publish time and is not in the file, so it is written out below for whoever does the publishing.
  3. They open it. The first load takes longer than the rest — first contact provisions a pod.
  4. They copy the handle their own lobby shows them and send it to you, by any means at all. This step cannot be automated away: their identifier does not exist until they connect, and no directory can find them by name.
  5. You paste that handle into Invite above. A grant naming them is published on your pod and a notice goes to their inbox.
  6. They reload, see the invitation checked against the grant on your pod, and press Accept. That is one write, to their own pod, with their own credentials — you do not and cannot do it for them.
  7. They post. Both pages now fold both logs.

Publishing this page

This page reads and writes only through a connector, and which tools it may call is decided when the page is published — not by anything inside the file. So the list is here, in the page, for whoever publishes the next copy of it.

Publish this file with the mcp capability and these eleven tool names as its manifest. All eleven are called: a copy published with fewer fails at the first call it is missing, which looks like a connector problem and is not one.
Two things this page cannot do for you, and says so rather than pretending otherwise: it cannot request a tool it was not granted, and it cannot be shared as a link — the connector grant belongs to the viewer, so a second person publishes their own copy and it runs on their credentials, against their pod.
